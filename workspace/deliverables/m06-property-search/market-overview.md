## Executive Summary

Portland's residential real estate market in early 2026 presents a **moderate opportunity window** for a fix-and-flip cooperative. After the correction of 2022-2023 and the rate-driven slowdown of 2024, prices have stabilized and begun a modest recovery. Distressed inventory remains elevated relative to 2019-2021 levels, particularly in outer East Portland and North Portland neighborhoods where deferred maintenance on aging housing stock is creating value-add opportunities.

**Key finding:** The sweet spot for the co-op is **inner East Portland neighborhoods** (Lents, Montavilla, Parkrose) where entry prices are 20-35% below metro median but appreciation trends are outpacing the metro. Margins are tighter in gentrifying areas (Foster-Powell, Woodstock) where entry prices have risen faster than ARVs can justify.

## Distressed Property Landscape

### Sources of Distressed Inventory

1. **Deferred maintenance / aging stock** — The primary source. Portland's inner East side was developed in the 1940s-1960s. These homes are now 60-80 years old. Original roofs, knob-and-tube wiring, galvanized plumbing, and failing foundations are common. Owners who can't afford $50K-$100K in updates sell at a discount or let properties deteriorate until they hit code enforcement.

2. **Estate sales** — Portland's Boomer homeowners are aging out. Properties inherited by out-of-state heirs often sell as-is at 65-80% of market value. These are our best targets: clean title, motivated sellers, no occupant complications.

3. **REO / bank-owned** — Post-forbearance foreclosures have stabilized but remain above 2019 levels. Banks are willing to negotiate, especially on properties sitting 60+ days.

4. **Code violation properties** — Multnomah County's code enforcement has become more aggressive. Properties with active violations often sell at steep discounts. Risk: violations can indicate deeper (more expensive) issues.

5. **Tax-delinquent properties** — Properties 2+ years delinquent are flagged for tax lien sale. These can be identified through county records but require careful title work (Statton will need to review).

## Competitive Landscape

### Who We're Competing Against

1. **Institutional flippers (Opendoor, Offerpad remnants)** — Largely pulled back from Portland. Not active in sub-$400K distressed space. **Low threat.**

2. **Local flip operators** — 15-20 active operators in Portland. Most focus on higher-end flips ($500K+ ARV) in inner Portland. A few work the outer East side. **Moderate threat.** Key names: Everett Custom Homes, PDX Property Group, Willamette Ventures.

3. **Buy-and-hold investors** — Actively competing for the same inventory, especially in Lents and Cully where rental yields are attractive. **Moderate threat.** They'll pay more than we want to because their math works on rental income.

4. **First-time homebuyers (FHA 203k)** — Buyers using renovation loans. They're slow (60-90 day close) which gives us an advantage with cash/fast-close offers. **Low threat on acquisition, strong demand on exit** — these are our target buyers.

### The 70% Rule (Our Guardrail)
Maximum purchase price = (ARV x 0.70) - Rehab Cost

Example: ARV $400K → Max purchase = ($400K x 0.70) - $75K rehab = **$205,000**

This rule ensures minimum 30% gross margin to cover holding costs, transaction costs, and profit. Deals that don't meet this threshold get rejected, period.

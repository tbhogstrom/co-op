## 1. Vision Statement

To build a worker-owned housing cooperative in Portland, Oregon that empowers skilled tradespeople to collectively acquire, rehabilitate, and sell residential properties — creating wealth, building community, and delivering quality housing.

## 2. Mission Statement

The Portland Housing Co-op buys distressed residential properties in Portland-area neighborhoods, renovates them using the combined skills of its member-owners, and sells them at market value. Profits are distributed equitably among members based on their contribution of labor, capital, and expertise. We operate transparently, share risk and reward, and reinvest in both our members and our community.

## 3. Business Model

### How It Works

1. **Capitalize**: Members buy in with an equity share. Combined with any external financing, this creates our acquisition fund.
2. **Acquire**: We identify undervalued or distressed residential properties (single-family homes, duplexes) in Portland neighborhoods with strong fundamentals.
3. **Rehabilitate**: Our member-tradespeople (carpenter, roofer, plumber, plus subcontracted specialists as needed) perform the renovation. A general contractor member manages the project.
4. **Sell**: We list the renovated property at market value.
5. **Distribute**: Net profit is split among members according to a formula that accounts for capital contribution, labor hours, and role.
6. **Reinvest**: A percentage of profit goes back into the co-op's capital reserve for the next deal.

### Revenue Model

- **Primary revenue**: Profit on property sale (ARV minus acquisition cost, rehab cost, carrying costs, transaction costs)
- **Target margin**: 15-25% net profit on total project cost
- **Deal frequency**: 2-4 properties per year once operational (starting with 1)

### Target Properties

- **Type**: Single-family homes, duplexes, small multifamily (2-4 units)
- **Condition**: Distressed, deferred maintenance, cosmetic/structural rehab needed
- **Price range**: $200,000 - $400,000 acquisition cost (Portland market 2026)
- **ARV range**: $350,000 - $600,000
- **Neighborhoods**: Emerging or stabilizing Portland neighborhoods (Lents, Foster-Powell, Cully, St. Johns, Woodstock)

## 4. Capitalization Target (Initial Proposal)

### Assumptions
- First deal acquisition: ~$300,000
- Rehab budget: ~$75,000 - $100,000
- Carrying costs (6-month project): ~$15,000 - $20,000
- Transaction costs (closing, commissions): ~$25,000 - $30,000
- **Total capital needed for first deal**: ~$415,000 - $450,000

### Funding Structure (Proposed)
- **Member equity buy-in**: $15,000 - $25,000 per member
- **Target membership**: 6-8 founding members
- **Total member equity**: $90,000 - $200,000
- **External financing**: Conventional investment property loan or hard money (60-75% LTV)
- **Required cash at closing**: ~$120,000 - $150,000 (down payment + rehab reserve + working capital)

## 5. Membership Structure (Initial Proposal)

### Member Classes
1. **Working Members**: Tradespeople who contribute labor AND capital (full profit share)
2. **Investing Members**: Contributors of capital only (profit share proportional to investment, no labor credit)
3. **Founding Members**: The initial leadership team — carry governance authority through M5

### Governance
- **M1-M5**: Maven directs, with input from all members
- **M6+**: Democratic governance. One member, one vote. Maven transitions to facilitator.
- **Decisions**: Simple majority for operations, 2/3 supermajority for capital allocation over $50,000, unanimous for dissolution

### Profit Distribution (Concept)
- **Capital return**: Members receive a preferred return on their equity investment (e.g., 8% annualized)
- **Labor credit**: Hours worked are tracked and valued at agreed rates per trade
- **Residual split**: After capital return and labor credit, remaining profit split equally among all working members
- **Co-op reserve**: 15-20% of net profit retained for next acquisition

## 6. Competitive Advantages

1. **Eliminated labor markup**: No paying retail for trades labor — our members ARE the labor force
2. **Aligned incentives**: Every worker has skin in the game and benefits from quality work
3. **Collective purchasing power**: Bulk materials purchasing, shared tools
4. **Knowledge compound**: Each project makes the team better; institutional knowledge stays in the co-op
5. **Portland market fit**: Strong demand for renovated homes, limited skilled-trade capacity, growing co-op culture

## 7. Risks & Open Questions

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Member disputes over profit split | Clear formula defined in operating agreement (Statton) |
| Project cost overruns | Conservative rehab budgets with 15% contingency (Ledger) |
| Market downturn | Target properties with strong fundamentals; don't over-leverage |
| Member attrition mid-project | Buyout provisions in membership agreement; cross-training |
| Licensing/insurance gaps | Verify all member licenses; co-op carries umbrella policy (Statton) |
| Cash flow timing | Hard money bridge financing if needed; stagger projects |

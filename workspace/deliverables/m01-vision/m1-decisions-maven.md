## Decision 1: Founding Member Count — 6

**Start with 6 founding members.** Easier to recruit, easier to manage, faster to capitalize. We can add members for flip 2+ once we have a track record.

Rationale: Calloway's recruitment brief is realistic — finding 6 skilled, licensed, financially stable tradespeople who also want to be co-op members is hard enough. Trying for 8 before flip 1 risks delaying the entire operation. If we end up with 7-8 strong candidates, great, but we don't hold the project for them.

## Decision 2: Capitalization Target — $200,000

**Ledger's $200K target is approved.** The model shows we never go below $263K cash balance in the conservative scenario. The $47K buffer is the right call.

I'm revising my original $415K-$450K estimate downward. That was before properly accounting for hard money leverage at 85% LTV. Ledger's capital stack (member equity + hard money) is the right structure.

## Decision 3: Member Buy-In Structure — Tiered, No Payment Plans

Adopting Ledger's 3-tier structure:
- **Tier A (Full Share):** $25,000 - $50,000
- **Tier B (Working Share):** $10,000 - $24,999
- **Tier C (Sweat Equity Entry):** $5,000

**Minimum buy-in: $5,000.** Below that, administrative overhead isn't worth it.
**Maximum buy-in: $50,000.** No single member should have outsized financial leverage in a democratic co-op.
**No payment plans for founding members.** We need the cash in the bank before we start looking at properties. Members 7+ (if any) may be offered installment terms.

## Decision 4: My Personal Capital Commitment — $50,000

I'm putting in the max. The founder needs to lead from the front. This represents 25% of total capitalization in a 6-member scenario.

## Decision 5: Entity Structure — Cooperative LLC

**Statton's "Cooperative LLC" hybrid is the right call.** Form as an Oregon LLC under ORS Chapter 63, with the Operating Agreement encoding cooperative principles:
- One member, one vote (by agreement)
- Patronage-based profit allocation (by agreement)
- Reserve requirements and democratic governance built into the OA
- Optional Subchapter T election (Statton to advise on timing)

Rationale: This gives us the governance philosophy of a cooperative with the tax flexibility and formation simplicity of an LLC. Lenders understand LLCs. The CCB understands LLCs. We can still call ourselves a housing co-op — we just can't use "cooperative" in the registered entity name.

If we grow to 15+ members and want the full cooperative identity, we can convert to an ORS Chapter 62 Cooperative Corporation in Year 3+. Not worth the overhead now.

## Decision 6: Profit Split Formula — Approved as Proposed

Ledger's formula is adopted:
- **20% to Reserves** — sacred, non-negotiable, funds the next deal
- **10% to Overhead Recovery** — reimburses co-op for insurance, admin, tools
- **30% to Capital Contributors** — proportional to each member's equity account
- **40% to Labor Contributors** — proportional to tracked labor hours, weighted by trade rate

The 40% labor weighting is the core value proposition for recruiting tradespeople. This is how we differentiate from a traditional investment syndicate.

## Decision 7: Labor Compensation During Rehab — Advance Against Share

**Members working full-time on the rehab may draw an advance of up to 50% of their estimated labor share.** This advance is deducted from their distribution at sale.

Rationale: Zero-draw (sweat equity only) is theoretically clean but practically brutal. Members who are working 40+ hours/week on the rehab need some cash flow. But we can't afford to pay market-rate stipends during the project — it would blow up the budget. The advance model preserves the economics while acknowledging reality.

Advances are:
- Optional (members who don't need cash flow don't take one)
- Capped at 50% of estimated labor share
- Deducted from distribution
- Only available during active rehab (not during sourcing, selling, or idle periods)
- Subject to board approval for amounts over $3,000/month

## Decision 8: Governance Structure

- **One member, one vote** — no exceptions, no weighted voting, no founder veto
- **Simple majority** for day-to-day operations and project decisions
- **2/3 supermajority** for: property acquisition, capital allocation >$50K, new member admission, member expulsion
- **Unanimous** for: dissolution, amendment of profit-split formula, conversion of entity type
- **M1-M5**: Maven directs operations, with input from all. This is practical, not philosophical — someone has to drive.
- **M6+**: Full democratic governance. Maven facilitates, doesn't dictate.

No founding member protections. No veto power. No preferred returns. We build trust by sharing risk equally. If I want to lead, I lead by being right and by showing up, not by structural privilege.

## Decision 10: First Flip Timeline — Month 4-5 Purchase

Revised from Ledger's Month 3 estimate:
- **Months 1-2**: Entity formation, capitalization, member onboarding
- **Month 3**: Begin property search (Reeves activates pipeline)
- **Months 4-5**: Identify and close on first property
- **Months 5-10**: Rehab (5-month target)
- **Months 10-12**: List and sell
- **Month 12-14**: Close sale, distribute profits

Realistic first distribution: 12-14 months from Day 1. Calloway should set this expectation clearly in recruitment materials.

## Decision 11: Minimum Deal Standards

- Purchase at **≤65% of ARV** (70% rule: max purchase = ARV × 0.70 − rehab cost)
- Target ROI **≥15%** on deployed capital (conservative scenario)
- Rehab budget **≤25% of ARV**
- Hold period target **≤6 months**
- **Never deploy >70% of co-op cash** on a single project
- **Kill a deal rather than stretch** — there are always more houses

## Decision 12: CCB Licensing & Insurance — Non-Negotiable Requirements

Per Statton's CCB research:
- Co-op entity must hold its own CCB General Contractor (Residential) license
- All members performing trade work must hold individual CCB licenses
- Members structured as independent contractor-members (not employees)
- EPA RRP certification required for all members (Portland's pre-1978 housing stock)
- GL insurance minimum $500K/$1M per member, plus co-op umbrella policy
- Workers comp analysis to be finalized by Statton in M2

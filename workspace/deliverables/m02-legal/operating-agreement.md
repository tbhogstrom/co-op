## RECITALS

WHEREAS, the Company has been formed as an Oregon limited liability company by the filing of Articles of Organization with the Oregon Secretary of State pursuant to ORS Chapter 63;

WHEREAS, the Members desire to establish a worker-owned cooperative enterprise for the purpose of acquiring, renovating, and selling residential real property in the Portland, Oregon metropolitan area;

WHEREAS, the Members intend to operate the Company in accordance with cooperative principles, including democratic governance, equitable distribution of surplus based on patronage (capital contribution and labor), and open membership;

WHEREAS, the Members desire to set forth their respective rights, duties, and obligations with respect to the Company;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants and agreements herein contained and for other good and valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged, the Members agree as follows:

## ARTICLE I — DEFINITIONS

As used in this Agreement, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:

**1.1 "Active Project"** means any real property owned by the Company on which renovation, rehabilitation, or construction work is in progress or planned, from the date of acquisition through the date of final sale closing.

**1.2 "Agreement"** means this Operating Agreement, as amended from time to time.

**1.3 "Articles"** means the Articles of Organization of the Company filed with the Oregon Secretary of State.

**1.4 "Board"** means the Board of Managers established pursuant to Article VII.

**1.5 "Capital Account"** means the individual capital account maintained for each Member pursuant to Article IV.

**1.6 "Capital Contribution"** means the total amount of cash or property contributed by a Member to the Company.

**1.7 "Capital Share"** means the portion of Distributable Profit allocated to Members proportionally based on their Capital Account balances, as set forth in Section 8.2(c).

**1.8 "CCB"** means the Oregon Construction Contractors Board.

**1.9 "Company"** means Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC, an Oregon limited liability company.

**1.10 "Distributable Profit"** has the meaning set forth in Section 8.2.

**1.11 "Effective Date"** means the date first set forth above.

**1.12 "Fiscal Year"** means the calendar year.

**1.13 "Gross Profit"** means, with respect to any Project, the excess of Sale Proceeds over Total Project Costs, as determined by the Treasurer in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles applied consistently.

**1.14 "Labor Advance"** means a cash payment to a Member during a Project's rehabilitation period, as set forth in Section 8.4.

**1.15 "Labor Hours"** means hours of labor contributed by a Member to a Project, tracked and verified in accordance with Section 8.3.

**1.16 "Labor Share"** means the portion of Distributable Profit allocated to Members proportionally based on their Weighted Labor Hours, as set forth in Section 8.2(d).

**1.17 "Majority Vote"** means the affirmative vote of more than fifty percent (50%) of all Members entitled to vote.

**1.18 "Member"** means any person admitted to the Company as a member in accordance with Article V and listed on Exhibit A, as amended from time to time.

**1.19 "Member Agreement"** means the separate agreement between the Company and each Member setting forth the terms of the Member's engagement, as described in Section 5.6.

**1.20 "Membership Interest"** means a Member's entire interest in the Company, including the Member's Capital Account, right to allocations and distributions, and governance rights.

**1.21 "Project"** means the acquisition, renovation, and sale of a single residential property by the Company.

**1.22 "Responsible Managing Individual" or "RMI"** means the Member designated by the Board to serve as the Company's responsible managing individual for CCB licensing purposes.

**1.23 "Sale Proceeds"** means the gross proceeds received by the Company from the sale of a Project property, less selling costs (commissions, closing costs, title insurance, and staging costs).

**1.24 "Supermajority Vote"** means the affirmative vote of at least two-thirds (2/3) of all Members entitled to vote.

**1.25 "Total Project Costs"** means, with respect to any Project, the sum of: the purchase price, rehabilitation costs, acquisition costs (inspection, appraisal, title, origination), carrying costs (interest, taxes, insurance, utilities), permit costs, and all other direct costs attributable to the Project.

**1.26 "Trade Rate"** means the multiplier applied to a Member's Labor Hours based on the Member's trade classification, as set forth in Section 8.3(b).

**1.27 "Treasurer"** means the officer appointed pursuant to Section 7.8 with responsibility for financial management of the Company.

**1.28 "Unanimous Vote"** means the affirmative vote of one hundred percent (100%) of all Members entitled to vote.

**1.29 "Weighted Labor Hours"** means a Member's Labor Hours multiplied by the applicable Trade Rate.

## ARTICLE III — PURPOSE AND POWERS

**3.1 Purpose.** The Company is organized for the purpose of:

(a) Acquiring distressed residential real property in the Portland, Oregon metropolitan area;

(b) Renovating and improving such properties using the labor of its Members;

(c) Selling improved properties at market value;

(d) Distributing profits to Members in accordance with the profit allocation formula set forth in Article VIII;

(e) Operating as a worker-owned cooperative enterprise; and

(f) Engaging in any and all lawful business activities incidental to the foregoing.

**3.2 Powers.** The Company shall have all powers permitted under ORS Chapter 63, including without limitation the power to:

(a) Purchase, hold, improve, mortgage, lease, sell, and dispose of real and personal property;

(b) Enter into contracts, including construction contracts, purchase agreements, and loan agreements;

(c) Borrow money and grant security interests in Company assets;

(d) Obtain and maintain all licenses, permits, and registrations necessary for its operations, including CCB licensure pursuant to ORS 701.021;

(e) Sue and be sued in its own name;

(f) Hire employees, engage independent contractors, and enter into agreements with its Members for the performance of services;

(g) Obtain insurance of all types; and

(h) Do all things necessary or incidental to the foregoing.

**3.3 Cooperative Principles.** The Company shall operate in accordance with the following cooperative principles:

(a) **Democratic Member Control.** Each Member shall have one (1) vote on all matters submitted to a vote, regardless of Capital Contribution or Membership Interest percentage.

(b) **Member Economic Participation.** Members shall contribute equitably to, and democratically control, the capital of the Company. Surplus arising from the Company's operations shall be allocated in accordance with Article VIII.

(c) **Open Membership.** Membership shall be open to all persons who meet the qualifications set forth in Section 5.1 and who are willing to accept the responsibilities of membership, without arbitrary discrimination.

## ARTICLE IV — CAPITAL CONTRIBUTIONS AND ACCOUNTS

**4.1 Initial Capital Contributions.** Each founding Member shall make an initial Capital Contribution to the Company in the amount set forth opposite such Member's name on Exhibit A. Capital Contributions shall be made in cash or, with Board approval, in other property valued at fair market value as determined by the Board.

**4.2 Buy-In Tiers.** The minimum initial Capital Contribution shall be Five Thousand Dollars ($5,000). The maximum initial Capital Contribution shall be Fifty Thousand Dollars ($50,000). These limits may be amended by Supermajority Vote.

| Tier | Capital Contribution Range | Description |
|------|---------------------------|-------------|
| Tier A — Full Share | $25,000 – $50,000 | Full capital participation |
| Tier B — Working Share | $10,000 – $24,999 | Moderate capital participation |
| Tier C — Sweat Equity Entry | $5,000 | Minimum entry; primary wealth building via labor |

**4.3 Additional Capital Contributions.** No Member shall be required to make additional Capital Contributions beyond the initial contribution set forth in Exhibit A, except by Unanimous Vote. Members may voluntarily make additional Capital Contributions with Board approval, subject to the maximum set forth in Section 4.2.

**4.4 Capital Accounts.** The Company shall maintain a separate Capital Account for each Member. Each Member's Capital Account shall be:

(a) Credited with: (i) the Member's Capital Contributions; (ii) the Member's allocated share of Company income and gain;

(b) Debited with: (i) distributions to the Member; (ii) the Member's allocated share of Company losses and deductions.

Capital Accounts shall be maintained in accordance with the rules of Treasury Regulation Section 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv), as applicable to partnership taxation.

**4.5 No Interest on Capital.** No Member shall be entitled to interest on the Member's Capital Contribution or Capital Account balance.

**4.6 Return of Capital.** No Member shall have the right to demand or receive the return of the Member's Capital Contribution except upon withdrawal from the Company in accordance with Section 5.8 or dissolution of the Company in accordance with Article XII.

**4.7 Capital Withdrawal.**

(a) A Member may request withdrawal of all or a portion of the Member's Capital Account balance by providing ninety (90) days' prior written notice to the Board (the "Withdrawal Notice").

(b) **No withdrawal shall be permitted during an Active Project.** If the Company has an Active Project on the date the Withdrawal Notice would otherwise become effective, the effective date of the withdrawal shall be automatically extended to the date that is thirty (30) days after the closing of the sale of the Active Project.

(c) The Board may deny or defer a withdrawal request if payment would cause the Company's cash balance to fall below the Minimum Reserve (as defined by Board resolution; initially Twenty Thousand Dollars ($20,000)).

(d) Upon an effective withdrawal, the Company shall pay the withdrawing Member an amount equal to the Member's Capital Account balance as of the effective date of withdrawal. Payment shall be made within sixty (60) days of the effective date, or in installments over twelve (12) months if the Board determines that a lump-sum payment would create undue financial hardship for the Company.

## ARTICLE V — MEMBERSHIP

**5.1 Member Classes.** The Company shall have the following classes of Members:

(a) **Working Members.** Members who contribute both capital and labor to the Company. Working Members participate in Project renovation work, earn both Capital Share and Labor Share distributions, and must satisfy all licensing and insurance requirements set forth in Section 5.2. Working Members have full voting rights.

(b) **Investing Members.** Members who contribute capital but do not perform construction labor on Company Projects. Investing Members earn Capital Share distributions only (no Labor Share). Investing Members must execute this Agreement and a Member Agreement but are not required to hold a CCB license or trade-specific insurance. Investing Members have full voting rights.

(c) **Founding Members.** The Members listed on Exhibit A as of the Effective Date. Founding Members may be Working Members or Investing Members. Founding Members are not required to satisfy the admission vote requirement of Section 5.3(b) but must satisfy all other qualifications applicable to their member class.

**5.2 Qualifications — Working Members.** To be eligible for admission as a Working Member, a person must:

(a) Hold an active Oregon CCB license (general or specialty) appropriate to the Member's trade, in good standing with no unresolved disciplinary actions;

(b) Maintain general liability insurance with minimum limits of Five Hundred Thousand Dollars ($500,000) per occurrence and One Million Dollars ($1,000,000) aggregate;

(c) Maintain a current surety bond as required by the CCB for the Member's license type;

(d) Hold current EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) Rule certification (40 CFR 745, Subpart E) if the Member will perform renovation work on structures built before 1978;

(e) Make a Capital Contribution within the range set forth in Section 4.2;

(f) Execute this Agreement and a Member Agreement; and

(g) Be approved for admission by Supermajority Vote of the existing Members.

**5.2A Qualifications — Investing Members.** To be eligible for admission as an Investing Member, a person must satisfy Sections 5.2(e), 5.2(f), and 5.2(g) above. Investing Members are not required to hold a CCB license, trade insurance, surety bond, or EPA RRP certification.

**5.3 Admission of New Members.**

(a) After the Effective Date, new Members (Working or Investing) may be admitted by Supermajority Vote, provided the candidate satisfies all qualifications applicable to their member class.

(b) The Board shall establish an application and vetting process for prospective Members, including verification of CCB licensing, insurance, and bonding for Working Member applicants.

**5.4 No Founding Member Special Rights.** All Members, whether founding or subsequently admitted, shall have equal governance rights. No Member shall have veto power, weighted voting, preferred returns, or other special rights by virtue of being a founding Member. The Operational Director role (Section 7.6) is an operational function, not a governance privilege, and shall sunset as provided therein.

**5.5 Member Representations.** By executing this Agreement, each Member represents and warrants that:

(a) The Member is not an employee of the Company and does not expect to be treated as an employee;

(b) The Member is an independent business person holding the Member's own CCB license, insurance, and bonding;

(c) The Member understands that membership in the Company entails both the opportunity for profit and the risk of loss;

(d) The Member has had the opportunity to consult with independent legal and tax counsel before executing this Agreement.

**5.6 Member Agreement.** Each Member shall execute a separate Member Agreement that establishes the specific terms of the Member's engagement with the Company, including the Member's trade classification, Trade Rate, insurance obligations, and independent contractor status. The form of Member Agreement is attached hereto as Exhibit B or maintained as a separate document.

**5.7 Membership Register.** The Board shall maintain a register of all Members, including each Member's name, address, Capital Contribution, date of admission, CCB license number, and insurance policy information.

**5.8 Voluntary Withdrawal.** A Member may withdraw from the Company by providing ninety (90) days' prior written notice to the Board. Upon withdrawal:

(a) The withdrawing Member's Capital Account balance shall be paid in accordance with Section 4.7;

(b) The withdrawing Member shall be entitled to any accrued but unpaid distributions for completed Projects in which the Member contributed Labor Hours;

(c) The withdrawing Member shall have no further claim to Company profits or assets after the effective date of withdrawal;

(d) If the withdrawing Member has received Labor Advances for an Active Project, such advances shall be reconciled against the Member's final distribution from that Project. If the advances exceed the Member's final distribution, the Member shall repay the excess to the Company within sixty (60) days.

**5.9 Expulsion.**

(a) A Member may be expelled from the Company only for Cause by Supermajority Vote.

(b) **"Cause"** means: (i) material breach of this Agreement or the Member Agreement that remains uncured thirty (30) days after written notice; (ii) loss, suspension, or revocation of the Member's CCB license; (iii) loss or lapse of required insurance coverage that remains uncured fourteen (14) days after written notice; (iv) conviction of a felony or crime involving dishonesty; (v) willful misconduct or gross negligence in the performance of work on a Company Project; (vi) fraud, theft, or misappropriation of Company funds or property; or (vii) violation of workplace safety standards that creates a substantial risk of injury.

(c) **Due Process.** Before any vote on expulsion, the Member subject to expulsion shall receive: (i) written notice specifying the grounds for expulsion at least thirty (30) days before the scheduled vote; (ii) the opportunity to submit a written response to the Board; and (iii) the opportunity to be heard at a meeting of the Members before the vote is taken.

(d) If expelled, the Member's Capital Account shall be paid in accordance with Section 4.7(d) (installment terms). The expelled Member shall forfeit any Labor Share allocation for any Active Project that has not yet reached final sale closing.

## ARTICLE VI — GOVERNANCE — VOTING

**6.1 One Member, One Vote.** Each Member in good standing shall have one (1) vote on all matters submitted to a vote of the Members. Voting rights shall not be weighted by Capital Contribution, Capital Account balance, labor contribution, or any other factor.

**6.2 Voting Thresholds.** The following voting thresholds shall apply:

(a) **Majority Vote** (more than 50% of all Members) required for:
- Election of Board members
- Approval of annual budget
- Adoption of Company policies and procedures
- Selection of vendors and service providers
- Approval of change orders exceeding Two Thousand Dollars ($2,000) on an Active Project
- All matters not otherwise specified

(b) **Supermajority Vote** (at least 2/3 of all Members) required for:
- Acquisition of real property
- Capital expenditures or allocations exceeding Fifty Thousand Dollars ($50,000)
- Admission of new Members
- Expulsion of Members
- Amendment of this Agreement (except as set forth in subsection (c))
- Incurrence of debt exceeding One Hundred Thousand Dollars ($100,000)
- Selection of hard money lender for a Project
- Entering into any contract with a term exceeding twelve (12) months

(c) **Unanimous Vote** (100% of all Members) required for:
- Dissolution of the Company
- Amendment of the profit allocation formula set forth in Section 8.2
- Conversion of the Company to a different entity type
- Merger or consolidation with another entity
- Any action that would eliminate or restrict a Member's right to one vote

**6.3 Quorum.** A quorum for any meeting of the Members shall consist of two-thirds (2/3) of all Members.

**6.4 Meetings.** The Members shall hold regular meetings at least monthly. Special meetings may be called by the Board or by any two (2) Members upon seven (7) days' written notice. Meetings may be conducted in person or by electronic means (video conference, telephone).

**6.5 Action Without Meeting.** Any action that may be taken at a meeting of the Members may be taken without a meeting if the action is consented to in writing (including electronic communication) by the number of Members required for such action. Such written consent shall have the same force and effect as a vote at a meeting.

**6.6 Proxy Voting.** Proxy voting is not permitted.

## ARTICLE VII — GOVERNANCE — BOARD OF MANAGERS

**7.1 Board of Managers.** The Company shall be governed by a Board of Managers (the "Board") consisting of not fewer than three (3) Members elected by Majority Vote.

**7.2 Board Authority.** The Board shall manage the day-to-day business affairs of the Company, including:

(a) Managing Project operations, timelines, and budgets;

(b) Maintaining Company books and records;

(c) Managing banking and financial transactions within approved budgets;

(d) Overseeing compliance with CCB, insurance, and regulatory requirements;

(e) Hiring and terminating employees or service providers (within approved budgets);

(f) Executing contracts on behalf of the Company in amounts up to Fifty Thousand Dollars ($50,000); and

(g) Such other matters as may be delegated by the Members.

**7.3 Limitations on Board Authority.** The Board shall not, without the applicable vote of Members specified in Section 6.2:

(a) Acquire or dispose of real property;

(b) Incur debt exceeding One Hundred Thousand Dollars ($100,000);

(c) Make capital expenditures exceeding Fifty Thousand Dollars ($50,000);

(d) Admit or expel Members;

(e) Amend this Agreement; or

(f) Take any action requiring Supermajority or Unanimous Vote.

**7.4 Term.** Board members shall serve one-year terms and may be re-elected. Board members may be removed by Majority Vote at any time with or without cause.

**7.5 Board Meetings.** The Board shall meet at least monthly. Board meetings may be called by any Board member upon three (3) days' notice. A majority of Board members shall constitute a quorum for Board action.

**7.6 Initial Managing Member (M1–M5).** During the Company's startup phase (from formation through completion of the fifth milestone as defined in the Company's project plan), the Member designated as Maven shall serve as the Managing Member with authority to manage day-to-day operations, assign work, set priorities, and resolve operational disputes, subject to the Board's oversight and the Members' voting rights under Article VI. The Managing Member role during this phase is an operational function delegated by the Members, not a special governance right. This appointment shall sunset automatically upon the Board's declaration that Milestone 5 is complete.

**7.7 Elected Managing Member (M6+).** Upon completion of Milestone 5, the initial Managing Member appointment shall terminate. Thereafter, the Members shall elect a Managing Member annually by Majority Vote. The elected Managing Member shall manage day-to-day operations of the Company, subject to the Board's oversight and the voting requirements of Article VI. Any Member in good standing may be nominated. The Managing Member may be removed and replaced by Majority Vote at any time.

**7.8 Officers.** The Board shall appoint the following officers from among the Members:

(a) **Chair** — Presides over Member and Board meetings; serves as primary spokesperson.

(b) **Treasurer** — Oversees financial management; maintains Capital Accounts; prepares financial statements; coordinates with tax preparer. The Member serving as Treasurer should have financial or accounting expertise.

(c) **Secretary** — Maintains Company records, meeting minutes, and the Membership Register.

(d) **Responsible Managing Individual (RMI)** — Serves as the Company's RMI for CCB licensing purposes; must meet CCB qualifications. The RMI should have general contractor experience.

Officers shall serve at the pleasure of the Board and may be removed and replaced by Board action at any time.

## ARTICLE VIII — PROFIT ALLOCATION AND DISTRIBUTIONS

**8.1 Project-Based Accounting.** The Company shall maintain separate financial records for each Project. Gross Profit shall be calculated on a per-Project basis upon the closing of the sale of each Project property.

**8.2 Profit Allocation Formula.** Gross Profit from each Project shall be allocated as follows:

**(a) Reserve Contribution — Twenty Percent (20%)**

Twenty percent (20%) of Gross Profit shall be retained by the Company in its reserve fund. The reserve fund shall be used for: (i) down payments and acquisition costs for future Projects; (ii) operating overhead during periods between Projects; (iii) contingency for Project cost overruns; and (iv) such other purposes as the Board may determine.

**(b) Overhead Recovery — Ten Percent (10%)**

Ten percent (10%) of Gross Profit shall be retained by the Company to reimburse operating overhead expenses, including insurance, administration, shared tools and equipment, and other fixed costs.

**(c) Capital Share — Thirty Percent (30%)**

Thirty percent (30%) of Gross Profit shall be allocated to Members proportionally based on each Member's Capital Account balance as of the first day of the Project (the date the Company acquired the Project property). Each Member's Capital Share shall be calculated as:

> Member's Capital Share = 30% × Gross Profit × (Member's Capital Account Balance ÷ Total of All Members' Capital Account Balances)

**(d) Labor Share — Forty Percent (40%)**

Forty percent (40%) of Gross Profit shall be allocated to Members proportionally based on each Member's Weighted Labor Hours contributed to the Project. Each Member's Labor Share shall be calculated as:

> Member's Labor Share = 40% × Gross Profit × (Member's Weighted Labor Hours ÷ Total of All Members' Weighted Labor Hours for the Project)

**(e) Verification.** The sum of the allocations in subsections (a) through (d) shall equal one hundred percent (100%) of Gross Profit:

> 20% (Reserves) + 10% (Overhead) + 30% (Capital) + 40% (Labor) = 100%

**8.3 Labor Hour Tracking.**

(a) **Recording.** Each Member shall record Labor Hours daily in the Company's time-tracking system. Labor Hours shall include hours of on-site construction work, material procurement directly for a Project, and Project-related permit and inspection attendance. Labor Hours shall not include governance activities, meetings, or administrative work unless specifically approved by the Board.

(b) **Trade Rates.** The following Trade Rate multipliers shall apply to Labor Hours based on the Member's trade classification:

| Trade Classification | Trade Rate |
|---------------------|------------|
| Project Manager / General Contractor | 1.15x |
| Licensed Plumber | 1.2x |
| Licensed Electrician | 1.2x |
| Licensed HVAC Technician | 1.2x |
| Roofing | 1.1x |
| Carpenter / Framing | 1.0x |
| Painting / Finishing / Drywall | 1.0x |
| General Labor | 1.0x |

Trade Rates may be amended by Supermajority Vote. New trade classifications may be added by Majority Vote.

(c) **Verification.** Labor Hours shall be verified weekly by the Project manager (or, during M1–M5, the Operational Director). Disputed hours shall be resolved by the Board. The Treasurer shall maintain final records of all verified Labor Hours.

(d) **Minimum Contribution.** A Member who contributes zero (0) Labor Hours to a Project shall receive no Labor Share for that Project but shall still receive the Member's Capital Share (if any).

**8.4 Labor Advances.**

(a) During the rehabilitation period of an Active Project, a Member may request a Labor Advance from the Company of up to fifty percent (50%) of the Member's estimated Labor Share for that Project.

(b) Labor Advances shall be calculated based on the Member's verified Labor Hours to date, the applicable Trade Rate, the Treasurer's good-faith estimate of total Project Gross Profit (using conservative assumptions), and the formula in Section 8.2(d).

(c) **Board Approval.** Labor Advance requests not exceeding Three Thousand Dollars ($3,000) per month may be approved by the Treasurer. Labor Advance requests exceeding Three Thousand Dollars ($3,000) per month require Board approval.

(d) Labor Advances are exactly that — advances, not wages. They shall be deducted from the Member's final Labor Share distribution upon completion of the Project. If the Project results in a loss or the Member's final Labor Share is less than the total Labor Advances received, the Member shall repay the excess to the Company within sixty (60) days.

(e) The Board may approve or deny Labor Advance requests based on Company cash flow. No Labor Advance shall be made if it would cause the Company's cash balance to fall below the Minimum Reserve.

(f) **Labor Advances are not wages, salary, or compensation for employment.** They are advances against anticipated profit distributions to Members in their capacity as owners of the Company.

**8.5 Distributions.**

(a) Capital Share and Labor Share distributions shall be made within thirty (30) days of the closing of the sale of a Project property and the Company's receipt of sale proceeds.

(b) The Treasurer shall prepare a distribution statement for each Project showing: Gross Profit, the four-way allocation, each Member's Capital Share, each Member's Labor Share, any Labor Advance deductions, and each Member's net distribution.

(c) Distributions shall be made by direct deposit or check to each Member's designated account.

**8.6 Losses.** If a Project results in a loss (negative Gross Profit), the loss shall be allocated to Members proportionally based on Capital Account balances. No Member's Capital Account shall be reduced below zero. Losses shall not create an obligation for Members to make additional Capital Contributions.

**8.7 Tax Allocations.** For federal and state income tax purposes, items of income, gain, loss, deduction, and credit shall be allocated among the Members in accordance with their respective shares of Gross Profit (or loss) as determined under this Article VIII and in compliance with IRC Section 704(b) and the regulations thereunder. The Company shall furnish each Member with a Schedule K-1 (or equivalent) within seventy-five (75) days after the close of each Fiscal Year.

**8.8 Withholding.** The Company shall not withhold income taxes, FICA, or any other employment taxes from distributions to Members. Each Member is responsible for the Member's own federal and state tax obligations arising from the Member's distributive share of Company income.

## ARTICLE IX — BOOKS, RECORDS, AND REPORTING

**9.1 Books and Records.** The Company shall maintain complete and accurate books and records at its principal office, including:

(a) A current list of all Members with addresses and Capital Account balances;

(b) Copies of the Articles, this Agreement, and all amendments;

(c) Federal and state tax returns for the three (3) most recent Fiscal Years;

**9.2 Member Inspection Rights.** Each Member shall have the right to inspect and copy Company books and records at any reasonable time upon reasonable notice, in accordance with ORS 63.185.

**9.3 Financial Reporting.** The Treasurer shall provide:

(a) Monthly: Cash flow summary, project budget vs. actual, bank account balances;

(b) Per Project: Distribution statement within thirty (30) days of sale closing;

(c) Annually: Year-end financial statements (balance sheet, income statement, member capital account statements).

**9.4 Banking.** All Company funds shall be deposited in accounts at a federally insured financial institution designated by the Board. Withdrawals over Five Thousand Dollars ($5,000) shall require two (2) authorized signers (Board members).

## ARTICLE X — LICENSING, INSURANCE, AND COMPLIANCE

**10.1 Company CCB License.** The Company shall obtain and maintain a CCB General Contractor (Residential) license pursuant to ORS 701.021. The Board shall designate a Responsible Managing Individual (RMI) pursuant to Section 7.8(d).

**10.2 Company Insurance.** The Company shall obtain and maintain the following insurance coverage:

(a) **General Liability:** Minimum One Million Dollars ($1,000,000) per occurrence, Two Million Dollars ($2,000,000) aggregate;

(b) **Workers Compensation:** Coverage for all persons performing work on Company Projects, as required by ORS Chapter 656 and as determined prudent by the Board, regardless of whether Members are technically exempt from mandatory coverage;

(c) **Umbrella / Excess Liability:** Minimum One Million Dollars ($1,000,000);

(d) **Builder's Risk:** Per-Project policy covering the full replacement cost of the property during renovation; and

(e) **Professional Liability / Errors and Omissions:** As recommended by the Company's insurance broker.

**10.3 Member Insurance.** Each Member shall, as a condition of membership, obtain and maintain:

(a) General liability insurance meeting CCB minimum requirements ($500,000/$1,000,000);

(b) A surety bond as required by the CCB for the Member's license type;

(c) Workers compensation coverage if the Member has any employees; and

(d) Occupational accident insurance (recommended) for the Member's own protection.

**10.4 Insurance Verification.** Each Member shall provide certificates of insurance and proof of bond to the Secretary annually and upon each renewal. Failure to maintain required insurance coverage is grounds for expulsion under Section 5.9(b)(iii).

**10.5 CCB Compliance.** All renovation work on Company Projects shall be performed by persons holding valid CCB licenses with endorsements appropriate to the work performed. The RMI shall verify CCB compliance before any Member begins work on a Project.

**10.6 EPA Lead-Based Paint Compliance.** For any Project involving a structure built before 1978, the Company shall comply with EPA RRP Rule requirements (40 CFR 745, Subpart E), including:

(a) The Company shall be registered as an EPA-certified renovation firm;

(b) At least one EPA-certified renovator shall be present on-site at all times during renovation work that disturbs painted surfaces; and

(c) All required lead-safe work practices shall be followed.

**10.7 Oregon OSHA.** All work on Company Projects shall comply with Oregon Occupational Safety and Health Act requirements (ORS Chapter 654, OAR 437). The Project manager or RMI shall conduct or designate safety oversight on each Project site.

## ARTICLE XI — TRANSFER AND ASSIGNMENT

**11.1 Restriction on Transfer.** No Member may sell, assign, transfer, pledge, or otherwise encumber all or any portion of the Member's Membership Interest without the prior written consent of a Supermajority of the other Members.

**11.2 Right of First Refusal.** Before transferring a Membership Interest to a third party, the transferring Member shall first offer the interest to the Company and then to the existing Members, on the same terms offered by the proposed transferee. The Company shall have thirty (30) days to accept, and, if the Company declines, the existing Members shall have an additional thirty (30) days to accept, pro rata.

**11.3 Involuntary Transfer.** In the event of a Member's death, disability, bankruptcy, or other involuntary transfer, the Company shall have the option to purchase the Member's Membership Interest at the Member's Capital Account balance as of the date of the triggering event. Payment shall be made within one hundred twenty (120) days or in installments over twelve (12) months, at the Board's election.

**11.4 No Transfer of Governance Rights Without Membership.** An assignee of economic rights (distributions) shall not become a Member or acquire voting or governance rights unless admitted as a Member pursuant to Section 5.3.

## ARTICLE XII — DISSOLUTION AND WINDING UP

**12.1 Events of Dissolution.** The Company shall be dissolved upon:

(a) Unanimous Vote of the Members;

(b) The occurrence of any event that makes it unlawful for the Company to continue its business;

(c) Entry of a judicial decree of dissolution under ORS 63.661; or

(d) The Company having fewer than two (2) Members for a period of ninety (90) consecutive days.

**12.2 Winding Up.** Upon dissolution, the Board (or a liquidating agent appointed by the Board) shall wind up the Company's affairs by:

(a) Completing or abandoning any Active Projects (Board to determine which course maximizes Member value);

(b) Collecting all debts owed to the Company;

(c) Selling or disposing of all remaining Company assets; and

(d) Distributing the net proceeds in the following order of priority:

> (i) First, to creditors of the Company (including Members who are creditors), in satisfaction of the Company's liabilities;
>
> (ii) Second, to Members in repayment of their Capital Account balances (pro rata if insufficient to repay in full);
>
> (iii) Third, any remaining surplus to Members based on cumulative patronage — calculated as fifty percent (50%) proportional to each Member's cumulative Capital Contributions over the life of the Company and fifty percent (50%) proportional to each Member's cumulative Weighted Labor Hours over the life of the Company. This patronage-based surplus distribution reflects the cooperative principle that surplus should be returned to Members in proportion to their participation in the enterprise.

**12.3 Filing.** Upon completion of winding up, the Board shall file Articles of Dissolution with the Oregon Secretary of State pursuant to ORS 63.644.

**12.4 No Obligation to Other Cooperatives.** Unlike an ORS Chapter 62 cooperative corporation, the Company is not required to distribute remaining assets to other cooperatives upon dissolution. All assets shall be distributed to Members as set forth in Section 12.2(d).

## ARTICLE XIII — INDEMNIFICATION AND LIABILITY

**13.1 Limitation of Liability.** No Member, Board member, or officer shall be liable to the Company or any Member for any act or omission taken in good faith and in a manner reasonably believed to be in or not opposed to the best interests of the Company, except for acts or omissions involving intentional misconduct, fraud, or a knowing violation of law.

**13.2 Indemnification.** The Company shall indemnify and hold harmless each Member, Board member, and officer from and against any loss, liability, damage, cost, or expense (including reasonable attorneys' fees) arising out of any act or omission performed in good faith on behalf of the Company, to the fullest extent permitted by ORS 63.160, except for acts or omissions involving intentional misconduct, fraud, or a knowing violation of law.

**13.3 Insurance.** The Company may obtain directors' and officers' liability insurance to fund the indemnification obligations of this Article XIII.

## ARTICLE XIV — DISPUTE RESOLUTION

**14.1 Internal Resolution.** Members shall first attempt to resolve disputes informally through discussion with the Board.

**14.2 Mediation.** If informal resolution is unsuccessful, the disputing parties shall submit the dispute to mediation administered by a mutually agreed mediator in Portland, Oregon. The Company shall bear the cost of mediation.

**14.3 Arbitration.** If mediation is unsuccessful within thirty (30) days, the dispute shall be submitted to binding arbitration in Portland, Oregon, administered by the Arbitration Service of Portland or similar local arbitration service, in accordance with the Oregon Uniform Arbitration Act (ORS Chapter 36). The arbitrator's decision shall be final and binding. Judgment on the award may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction.

**14.4 Governing Law.** This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Oregon, without regard to conflicts of law principles.

**14.5 Venue.** Any action or proceeding arising under this Agreement that is not subject to arbitration shall be brought exclusively in the Circuit Court of the State of Oregon for Multnomah County, or the United States District Court for the District of Oregon.

**14.6 Attorneys' Fees.** In any action, suit, arbitration, or proceeding to enforce or interpret this Agreement, the prevailing party shall be entitled to recover reasonable attorneys' fees and costs from the non-prevailing party.

## ARTICLE XV — INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR STATUS

**15.1 Member Status.** Each Member is an independent business person and owner of the Company. No Member is an employee of the Company. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to create an employer-employee relationship between the Company and any Member.

**15.2 No Employment Benefits.** Members shall not receive wages, salary, health insurance, retirement benefits, paid leave, unemployment insurance, or any other benefit customarily provided to employees. Members' compensation for participation in Company Projects consists solely of their distributive shares of Company profits (Capital Share and Labor Share) as set forth in Article VIII.

**15.3 Tax Responsibility.** Each Member is solely responsible for the payment of all federal and state income taxes, self-employment taxes, and any other taxes arising from the Member's distributive share of Company income. The Company shall not withhold taxes on behalf of Members.

**15.4 Member's Independent Business.** Each Member: (a) holds the Member's own CCB license; (b) maintains the Member's own insurance and bonding; (c) may perform work for other clients outside the Company; (d) provides the Member's own tools (except for shared Company tools); and (e) controls the manner and means of performing the Member's trade work, subject to Project-level coordination with the Project manager.

**15.5 Oregon ORS 670.600 Compliance.** The Company and each Member intend that the relationship satisfies the independent contractor factors set forth in ORS 670.600. The Operating Agreement, Member Agreements, and all Company practices shall be structured to support this classification.

**15.6 Non-Competition.** During the term of a Member's membership and for a period of six (6) months following voluntary withdrawal or expulsion:

(a) No Member shall, without prior written approval of the Board, individually or through any entity acquire, renovate, and sell (i.e., "flip") residential real property within the Portland metropolitan area (Multnomah, Clackamas, and Washington counties) for the purpose of resale at a profit.

(b) This restriction shall not apply to: (i) a Member's personal residence; (ii) performing trade work as an independent contractor for other persons or entities (Members are expressly permitted to maintain their own trade businesses and serve other clients); (iii) passive investment in real estate (e.g., rental properties, REITs); or (iv) real property transactions outside the Portland metropolitan area.

(c) The Board may waive this restriction on a case-by-case basis upon written request by the Member. The Board shall not unreasonably withhold such approval.

(d) **Risk Note:** Non-compete provisions for independent contractors (as opposed to employees) face heightened scrutiny under Oregon law. This provision is drafted narrowly — limited to the specific activity of house flipping, limited geography, and limited duration — to maximize enforceability. See ORS 653.295 (which applies to employees; this section is drafted to avoid triggering employee-focused non-compete statutes by applying only to owner-members).

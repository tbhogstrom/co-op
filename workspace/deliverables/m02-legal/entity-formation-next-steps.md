## Overview

This memo lists, in order, every step required to bring Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC from filed Articles of Organization to a fully licensed, insured, and operational entity. Each step includes what it is, who is responsible, estimated cost, estimated timeline, and dependencies.

**Estimated total timeline from OA execution to construction-ready: 8-10 weeks.**
**Estimated total entity-level formation costs: $4,500-$8,600 (Year 1).**

---

## Step 1: File Articles of Organization with Oregon Secretary of State

**What:** Submit the Articles of Organization online through the Oregon Business Registry to formally create Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC as a domestic Oregon LLC. The filing-ready version is at `articles-of-organization-filing.md` with step-by-step online filing instructions.

**Who:** Maven (Organizer). Statton reviews final submission.

**Cost:** $100 (online filing fee, payable by credit card or e-check)

**Timeline:** 15 minutes to file online; 2-3 business days for acceptance and issuance of Registry Number. Expedited processing available (~$50-$100 additional) for 1-2 day turnaround.

**Dependencies:** None. This is the first step. However, coordinate timing with Operating Agreement execution — the OA references the filing date in Section 2.1. Recommended approach: file Articles first, then execute the OA with the filing date filled in.

**Deliverable:** Oregon Secretary of State Registry Number; filed/stamped Articles confirmation.

**Authority:** ORS 63.044, 63.047, 63.054.

## Step 2: Execute the Operating Agreement

**What:** All founding Members sign the Operating Agreement. The OA becomes effective on the date of execution (Section 11 of the OA preamble). The filing date from Step 1 is entered in Section 2.1.

**Who:** All six founding Members. Maven coordinates execution. Statton confirms final version.

**Cost:** $0 (internal document)

**Timeline:** Same day as or within 1 week of Articles filing. Requires all Members to be available for signature (in person or via electronic signature).

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (Articles filed — need filing date and Registry Number for OA Section 2.1).

**Deliverable:** Fully executed Operating Agreement; signed copies to all Members; original maintained per OA Section 9.1(b).

## Step 3: Obtain Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)

**What:** Apply for a federal EIN from the IRS using Form SS-4. The EIN is required for bank account opening, tax filings, CCB licensing, and virtually all business operations. Apply online at https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online.

**Who:** Maven or Ledger (Treasurer). Either may apply as a "responsible party" for the LLC.

**Cost:** $0 (no IRS filing fee)

**Timeline:** Immediate. The IRS online EIN application issues the EIN instantly upon completion. The application takes approximately 10-15 minutes. Available Monday-Friday, 7:00 AM - 10:00 PM Eastern.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity must exist — need legal name and formation state). The IRS does not require the Registry Number, but the entity must be legally formed.

**Key information needed for Form SS-4:**
- Legal name: Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC
- State of organization: Oregon
- Date of organization: [filing date from Step 1]
- Entity type: LLC (taxed as partnership — do NOT elect corporate taxation)
- Number of members: 6 (initial)
- Principal business activity: Residential building construction (NAICS 236118)
- Responsible party: Maven [full legal name and SSN required]
- Mailing address: [Co-op's Portland address]

**Deliverable:** IRS EIN assignment letter (CP 575); EIN number.

**Authority:** IRC Section 6109; IRS Form SS-4 instructions.

## Step 4: Register with Oregon Department of Revenue

**What:** Register the Co-op with the Oregon Department of Revenue via the Combined Employer Registration (CER) form. This registers the entity for Oregon income tax withholding (if the Co-op later hires employees), state unemployment insurance, and workers' compensation reporting. Even if the Co-op initially has no W-2 employees (all Members are independent contractor-members), registration is advisable to establish the entity in the Oregon tax system and may be required when the Co-op later files its Oregon partnership return.

If the Co-op filed Articles online through the Oregon Business Registry, the entity may be partially registered automatically. Verify and complete registration at: https://www.oregon.gov/dor/Pages/index.aspx.

**Who:** Ledger (Treasurer). Maven assists with filing.

**Cost:** $0 (no registration fee)

**Timeline:** 1-2 weeks from EIN receipt. Can be done online.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed), Step 3 (EIN obtained).

**Deliverable:** Oregon Business Identification Number (BIN); confirmation of registration.

## Step 5: Open Business Bank Account

**What:** Open a business checking account at a federally insured financial institution for all Company funds. Per OA Section 9.4, all Company funds must be deposited in accounts at a federally insured institution designated by the Board. Withdrawals over $2,500 require two authorized signers (Board members).

**Who:** Ledger (Treasurer) and Maven. Both should be present at account opening. All Board members should be established as authorized signers.

**Cost:** $0-$50 (depending on institution; many business checking accounts have no opening fee with minimum deposit). Ongoing monthly fees vary by institution ($0-$30/month).

**Timeline:** 1-3 business days from EIN receipt. Schedule appointment at chosen institution. Some banks/credit unions allow online business account opening.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (filed Articles — bank will require a copy), Step 2 (executed OA — bank may require a copy or certification of authority), Step 3 (EIN — required for all business accounts).

**Requirements at account opening:**
- Filed Articles of Organization (stamped/confirmed copy)
- EIN assignment letter (IRS CP 575)
- Operating Agreement (or relevant excerpts showing authorized signers)
- Government-issued photo ID for all signers
- Initial deposit (amount varies by institution; recommend $500-$1,000)

**Account setup considerations (per OA Section 9.4):**
- Require dual signatures on checks and withdrawals over $2,500
- Set up online banking with appropriate access levels
- Consider a separate savings/reserve account for the 20% Reserve Contribution (OA Section 8.2(a))
- Confirm FDIC/NCUA insurance coverage

**Recommended institutions:** Consider Oregon-based credit unions (e.g., OnPoint, Unitus, Advantis) or community banks with experience serving LLCs and small businesses. Credit unions often have lower fees and more favorable terms for cooperatives.

**Deliverable:** Business checking account number; debit cards/checks for authorized signers; online banking access.

## Step 6: Obtain CCB General Contractor (Residential) License

**What:** Apply for an entity-level Construction Contractors Board license for Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC. This is mandatory before the Co-op performs any renovation or construction work (ORS 701.021). See `ccb-application-checklist.md` for the detailed application guide.

**Who:** Maven (coordinates application); designated RMI (completes individual qualification requirements); Statton (reviews application for legal compliance).

**Cost breakdown:**

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|------|---------------|
| CCB entity license application fee | ~$325 |
| Entity surety bond (annual premium) | ~$200-$500 |
| RMI pre-license training (16 hours, if needed) | ~$200-$400 |
| RMI CCB exam fee (if needed) | ~$75-$150 |
| **Subtotal — CCB licensing** | **~$800-$1,375** |

**Timeline:** 4-8 weeks total:
- Weeks 1-3: Designate RMI; RMI completes pre-license training and exam (if not already licensed)
- Weeks 2-4: Obtain entity surety bond ($20,000 residential)
- Week 4-5: File CCB application (all pre-requisites must be met)
- Weeks 5-8: CCB processes application (2-4 weeks typical)

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed — need Registry Number), Step 3 (EIN), Step 7 (general liability insurance — must be bound before CCB application), surety bond obtained, RMI qualified.

**Key requirements per OA Section 10.1 and CCB checklist:**
- License type: General Contractor, Residential
- Entity type: LLC
- Designated RMI per OA Section 7.8(d) — recommendation: Maven
- Entity surety bond: $20,000 (residential general contractor)
- Proof of general liability insurance (see Step 7)
- Proof of workers' compensation insurance or exemption (see Step 7)
- Copy of filed Articles of Organization

**Post-approval obligations:**
- Display CCB license number in all advertising, contracts, and at job sites (ORS 701.305)
- Renew every 2 years
- Report any changes in RMI, insurance, or bond within 30 days

**Deliverable:** CCB entity license number; license certificate.

**Authority:** ORS 701.021, 701.035, 701.305; OAR 812-002-0000 et seq.

## Step 7: Obtain Entity Insurance Coverage

**What:** Bind the required insurance policies for the entity. Per OA Section 10.2, the Co-op must maintain the following coverage. General liability and workers' compensation proof are also required for the CCB application (Step 6), so insurance must be obtained before or concurrently with the CCB application.

**Who:** Maven (coordinates with insurance broker); Ledger (reviews costs against budget); Board (approves policies and premium expenditures).

**Cost breakdown (annual premiums):**

| Coverage | OA Requirement | Estimated Annual Premium |
|----------|---------------|------------------------|
| General Liability (CGL) | $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate (Section 10.2(a)) | $2,500-$5,000 |
| Workers' Compensation | Per ORS Ch. 656; Board to determine scope (Section 10.2(b)) | $3,000-$8,000 (if elected; see note below) |
| Umbrella / Excess Liability | $1M (Section 10.2(c)) | $500-$1,500 |
| Builder's Risk | Per-project, full replacement cost (Section 10.2(d)) | Bound per project; $500-$2,000/project |
| Professional Liability / E&O | As recommended by broker (Section 10.2(e)) | $500-$1,500 |
| **Subtotal — entity insurance (Year 1, excl. builder's risk)** | | **$3,500-$8,000** |

> **Workers' Compensation note:** If all Members operate as independent contractor-members
> (not W-2 employees), workers' comp may not be legally mandatory (ORS 656.027 exempts
> LLC members). However, OA Section 10.2(b) directs the Board to obtain coverage
> "regardless of whether Members are technically exempt." Strongly recommended for risk
> management. If the Co-op hires any W-2 employees or day laborers, workers' comp
> becomes mandatory.

**Timeline:** 2-4 weeks from entity formation:
- Week 1: Engage insurance broker; request quotes
- Weeks 2-3: Review quotes; Board approves policies
- Week 3-4: Bind policies; obtain ACORD certificates

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed), Step 3 (EIN — insurers require it), Step 5 (bank account — for premium payments).

**Key actions:**
- Engage a commercial insurance broker experienced with construction/contractor coverage in Oregon
- Obtain ACORD certificates of insurance naming the CCB as certificate holder (for CCB application)
- Set up policy renewal reminders
- Verify all insurers are authorized to do business in Oregon

**Deliverable:** Bound insurance policies; ACORD certificates for each coverage line; certificate naming CCB as holder (for Step 6).

**Authority:** OA Section 10.2; ORS 701.073 (CCB insurance requirements); ORS Chapter 656 (workers' comp).

## Step 8: File Initial Oregon Annual Report

**What:** Oregon LLCs must file an annual report with the Secretary of State. The first annual report is due on the anniversary of the filing date. The report confirms the entity's current information (name, registered agent, principal office, members/managers). File online through the Oregon Business Registry.

**Who:** Ledger (Treasurer) or Maven.

**Cost:** $100 (annual report filing fee)

**Timeline:** Due on the first anniversary of the Articles filing date (i.e., approximately April 2027 if filed in April 2026). Set a calendar reminder 60 days in advance.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed). No other dependencies.

**Deliverable:** Filed annual report confirmation.

**Authority:** ORS 63.787.

---

## Step 9: Obtain Portland Business License

**What:** The City of Portland requires most businesses operating within city limits to hold a City of Portland Business License, administered through the Revenue Division. The license is technically a "Business License Tax" registration — Portland imposes a business license tax on net income above a threshold.

**Who:** Ledger (Treasurer). Maven assists.

**Cost:** $100/year (Portland business license fee for most small businesses; may vary based on gross revenue). The business license tax itself is 2.6% of net income apportioned to Portland (applies only if taxable income exceeds threshold; threshold is approximately $100,000 for partnerships as of 2025 — verify current threshold).

**Timeline:** 2-4 weeks. Register online at https://www.portland.gov/revenue/business-tax. Should be completed before the Co-op begins generating revenue in Portland.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed), Step 3 (EIN).

**Note:** Multnomah County also imposes a business income tax (BIT). Registration for the county tax is typically handled through the same Portland Revenue Division portal.

**Deliverable:** Portland Business License Tax registration confirmation; Multnomah County BIT registration.

**Authority:** Portland City Code Chapter 7.02; Multnomah County Business Income Tax Code.

---

## Step 10: Obtain EPA RRP Firm Certification

**What:** Portland's housing stock is predominantly pre-1978 construction. Before performing renovation work that disturbs painted surfaces in pre-1978 structures, the entity must be certified as an EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) firm under 40 CFR 745, Subpart E. Additionally, at least one EPA-certified renovator must be on-site during such work. See OA Section 10.6.

**Who:** Maven (files firm certification application); individual Members obtain EPA RRP Renovator certifications independently.

**Cost:**

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|------|---------------|
| EPA RRP Firm Certification application | ~$300 |
| EPA RRP Renovator training (per individual, 8-hour initial course) | ~$200-$300/person |

**Timeline:** 2-4 weeks for firm certification; individual renovator training courses are typically available within 1-2 weeks of enrollment.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed). May be pursued concurrently with Steps 6-7.

**Deliverable:** EPA RRP Firm Certification; individual EPA RRP Renovator certifications for designated Members.

**Authority:** 40 CFR 745, Subpart E; OA Section 10.6.

## Step 11: Register with Oregon Employment Department (if applicable)

**What:** If the Co-op hires any W-2 employees (as opposed to engaging Members as independent contractor-members), register with the Oregon Employment Department for state unemployment insurance (SUI) tax reporting. Under the current structure (all Members as independent contractors), this may not be immediately required, but should be completed before any employees are hired.

**Who:** Ledger (Treasurer).

**Cost:** $0 (no registration fee; SUI tax rate applies to wages paid — typically 1.5%-5.4% of taxable wages, varies by employer).

**Timeline:** Register before first payroll if employees are hired. Can be deferred if no W-2 employees.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed), Step 3 (EIN).

**Deliverable:** Oregon Employment Department account number.

## Step 12: Establish Accounting and Tax Compliance Infrastructure

**What:** Set up the bookkeeping, accounting, and tax filing systems required by the OA and applicable law. This is an operational step but is critical to legal compliance.

**Who:** Ledger (Treasurer) — primary responsibility. Maven assists. Engage a licensed Oregon CPA for Year 1 tax return preparation.

**Cost:** CPA engagement: $1,500-$3,000/year (for partnership return preparation, K-1s, and advisory). Accounting software: $0-$50/month (QuickBooks, Wave, or similar).

**Timeline:** Establish within 30 days of entity formation. CPA should be engaged before first revenue-generating activity.

**Dependencies:** Step 1 (entity formed), Step 3 (EIN), Step 5 (bank account opened).

**Key actions:**
- Select and set up accounting software
- Establish Chart of Accounts per OA Article IX requirements
- Set up project-level cost tracking (OA Section 8.1, 9.1(e))
- Establish labor hour tracking system (OA Section 8.3)
- Engage CPA for Year 1 tax compliance (Form 1065, Oregon partnership return, K-1s)
- Set up estimated tax payment reminders (federal and Oregon)
- Per tax memo recommendation: defer PTE election in Year 1; do not elect S-corp (see `tax-structure-memo.md`)

**Deliverable:** Accounting system operational; CPA engaged; Chart of Accounts established.

**Authority:** OA Article IX (Books, Records, and Reporting); OA Section 8.7 (Tax Allocations).

## Summary: Sequencing and Critical Path

```
Week 0 (Day 1)
  |-- Step 1: File Articles of Organization ($100)
  |-- Step 2: Execute Operating Agreement ($0)
  |
Week 0-1
  |-- Step 3: Obtain EIN ($0) -- immediate upon filing
  |-- Step 4: Register with OR Dept of Revenue ($0)
  |
Week 1-2
  |-- Step 5: Open business bank account ($0-$50)
  |-- Step 9: Register for Portland business license ($100)
  |-- Step 12: Set up accounting infrastructure ($0-$50/month + CPA)
  |-- Begin RMI qualification process (if needed)
  |
Week 2-4
  |-- Step 7: Obtain entity insurance ($3,500-$8,000/year)
  |   (Must be completed before CCB application)
  |-- Step 10: File EPA RRP Firm Certification ($300)
  |-- Obtain entity surety bond ($200-$500)
  |
Week 4-5
  |-- Step 6: File CCB application (~$325 + bond + RMI costs)
  |   (Requires: EIN, insurance, bond, RMI qualification)
  |
Week 5-8
  |-- CCB processes application (2-4 weeks)
  |
Week 8-10
  |-- Step 6 complete: CCB license issued
  |-- ENTITY IS CONSTRUCTION-READY
  |
Ongoing / Deferred
  |-- Step 8: Annual report (due on filing anniversary, ~April 2027)
  |-- Step 11: Employment Dept registration (when/if employees hired)
```

**Critical path:** The longest sequential dependency chain is:
Articles filed --> EIN obtained --> Insurance bound --> CCB application filed --> CCB license issued.

The CCB license is the gating item. Everything else can be completed in parallel.

## 1. Recommendation

**The Portland Housing Co-op shall be organized as an Oregon Limited Liability Company under ORS Chapter 63, with cooperative governance principles encoded in the Operating Agreement (the "Cooperative LLC" model).**

Entity Name: **Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC** (pending Secretary of State name availability)
Alternate Names: Portland Housing Co-op, LLC; PDX Housing Cooperative, LLC

## 2. Rationale

This recommendation was developed through preliminary research (see `entity-comparison-research.md`) and finalized based on M1 decisions by Maven (governance) and Ledger (financial structure).

### Why LLC Over ORS Chapter 62 Cooperative Corporation

| Factor | ORS 62 Cooperative | ORS 63 LLC (Selected) |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Profit allocation | Patronage only (Subchapter T) | **Fully customizable** — accommodates Ledger's 20/10/30/40 formula |
| Taxation | Entity-level tax on retained earnings | **Default pass-through** — no entity-level tax on reserves |
| Formation | Minimum 3 incorporators | **1 organizer** — Maven can file immediately |
| Governance flexibility | Rigid one-member-one-vote by statute | **One-member-one-vote by agreement** — same outcome, more flexibility |
| Dissolution | Statutory distribution to other cooperatives | **Operating Agreement controls** — full flexibility |
| Lender familiarity | Medium | **High** — banks, title companies, hard money lenders all work with LLCs daily |

### The Critical Tax Issue

Ledger's reserve policy allocates 20% of gross profit to co-op reserves. Under ORS Chapter 62 (cooperative corporation), retained earnings not distributed as patronage dividends are taxed at the entity level (corporate income tax). Under the LLC's default partnership treatment, all income passes through to members on K-1s regardless of whether it is distributed. This eliminates a significant tax friction that would penalize the co-op's reserve-building strategy.

### Cooperative Identity Preserved

Although the entity is legally an LLC, the Operating Agreement encodes:
- One member, one vote (Article VI)
- Patronage-style profit allocation weighted by labor contribution (Article VIII)
- Democratic governance with supermajority protections (Article VII)
- Member admission and expulsion procedures with due process (Article V)
- Dissolution provisions prioritizing member capital accounts (Article XII)

**Note on naming:** ORS 62.805(1) restricts the use of "cooperative" in entity names to entities formed under ORS Chapter 62. However, the restriction applies to use of the word "cooperative" as a corporate designation (like "Inc." or "Corp."). Using "Cooperative" as part of a descriptive LLC name (e.g., "Portland Housing Cooperative, LLC") is permissible because the LLC designation is included. If the Secretary of State rejects the name, we will use "Portland Housing Co-op, LLC" as the alternative. **Risk (LOW):** Possible name rejection; easily mitigated with alternate names.

## 3. Tax Election

**Default partnership taxation (IRS Form 1065).** No S-corp or C-corp election at this time.

Rationale:
- Pass-through treatment eliminates entity-level tax
- Members report income on individual returns via Schedule K-1
- Self-employment tax applies to members' distributive shares, but this is the expected outcome for owner-operators contributing labor
- Oregon PTE-E (pass-through entity tax under ORS 314.990) available if beneficial — Ledger to evaluate during first tax year
- Subchapter T election not pursued (unsettled law for LLCs; unnecessary given pass-through default)

**Coordination with Ledger:** Ledger should model Year 1 member tax obligations (federal SE tax + Oregon income tax on K-1 distributive shares) to set member expectations.

## 3. Who Must Be Licensed?

### The Co-op Entity

Under ORS 701.021(1), **any person who, for compensation, arranges or undertakes or offers to undertake or submits a bid to construct, alter, repair, add to, subtract from, improve, move, wreck, or demolish** any building or structure must hold a valid CCB license.

If the co-op:
- Purchases a property
- Renovates it using member labor
- Sells it for profit

Then the co-op entity **shall** hold a CCB license. The co-op is acting as a general contractor with respect to the renovation work.

**License type needed:** General Contractor (residential), Category: Residential General Contractor

### Individual Members

This is where it gets nuanced:

**Scenario A — Members as employees of the co-op:**
- Members do NOT need individual CCB licenses if working under the co-op's license
- The co-op is responsible for workers' compensation and payroll taxes
- **Risk (HIGH):** This creates an employment relationship that conflicts with the cooperative member model

**Scenario B — Members as independent subcontractors:**
- Each member performing specialty trade work (plumbing, electrical, roofing, etc.) **must** hold their own CCB license with the appropriate endorsement
- Each member must carry their own insurance and bond
- The co-op acts as general contractor; members are subs
- **This is the expected model for the co-op**

**Scenario C — Members as co-op owners performing their own work:**
- Under ORS 701.026(1)(a), an owner of property who performs work on their own property is exempt from CCB licensing — BUT this exemption does not apply if the **intent is to sell** the property
- ORS 701.026(1)(a) specifically excludes the exemption when the work is performed "with intent to offer the structure for sale"
- **Therefore, the owner-builder exemption does NOT apply to this co-op's model.** Every member performing licensed work must hold a CCB license.

## 4. CCB License Requirements (Individual)

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Application | File with CCB; fee varies by license type |
| Bond | Surety bond required: $20,000 for residential contractors, $75,000 for commercial |
| Insurance — General Liability | Minimum $500,000 per occurrence / $1,000,000 aggregate (OAR 812-003-0175) |
| Insurance — Workers Comp | Required if contractor has employees; exempt if sole proprietor with no employees |
| Continuing Education | Required for license renewal (OAR 812-020) |
| Lead-based Paint | EPA RRP Rule certification required for work on pre-1978 homes (40 CFR 745) — highly relevant for Portland housing stock |
| Specialty Endorsements | Plumbing, electrical, HVAC require separate state licenses in addition to CCB |

## 6. Worker Classification — Critical Issue

**Risk Rating: HIGH**

The interplay between CCB licensing and worker classification is the single most legally complex issue for this co-op. Here is the problem:

### The Tension

1. If members are **employees** of the co-op → co-op must provide workers comp → members don't need individual CCB licenses → but this undermines the cooperative model (members become employees, not owners)

2. If members are **independent contractors** → each must have own CCB license, bond, and insurance → co-op doesn't provide workers comp → but this preserves the cooperative member model

3. If members are **bona fide cooperative members** (neither employee nor independent contractor) → Oregon law is unclear on workers comp obligations → CCB licensing still required for the entity and potentially for individual members

### Oregon's Test for Independent Contractor Status

ORS 670.600 provides a multi-factor test. Key factors:

1. The worker is free from direction and control (co-op members making collective decisions may satisfy this)
2. The worker is customarily engaged in an independently established business (CCB-licensed tradespeople likely satisfy this)
3. The worker is responsible for obtaining their own licenses
4. The worker bears the risk of loss (co-op profit sharing complicates this — members share in losses too)

### My Preliminary Recommendation

Structure members as **independent contractor-members** who:
- Hold their own CCB licenses
- Carry their own GL insurance and bonds
- Contribute labor to the co-op under a Member Agreement (not an employment contract)
- Share in profits and losses through the Operating Agreement
- Have governance rights (voting, board participation)

This positions members as something between independent contractors and traditional co-op members. The Operating Agreement and Member Agreement must be carefully drafted to support this classification.

## 7. Recruitment Criteria for Calloway

Based on this research, I recommend the following **minimum requirements** for co-op membership, which Calloway should incorporate into recruitment materials:

### Mandatory

1. Active Oregon CCB license (general or specialty, as applicable to trade)
2. Current general liability insurance meeting CCB minimums ($500K/$1M)
3. Current surety bond (per CCB requirements for license type)
4. Lead-based paint certification (EPA RRP Rule) — required for working on pre-1978 homes
5. No active CCB complaints or disciplinary actions (verified via CCB online lookup)
6. Oregon residency or principal place of business in Oregon

### Strongly Recommended

7. Minimum 3 years of experience in relevant trade
8. Workers compensation coverage (if member has any employees of their own)
9. Professional references from previous clients or contractors
10. Clean criminal background (relevant to bonding and insurance eligibility)

## 8. Lead-Based Paint — Special Note for Portland

**Risk Rating: MEDIUM**

Portland's housing stock is predominantly pre-1978. Under the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) Rule (40 CFR 745, Subpart E):

- Any contractor disturbing more than 6 square feet of painted surface in a pre-1978 home must be EPA-certified
- The contracting firm must be an EPA-certified renovation firm
- At least one certified renovator must be on-site during work
- Specific work practices (containment, HEPA vacuuming, wet methods) are required
- Penalties for non-compliance: up to $37,500 per day per violation (as adjusted for inflation)

**The co-op entity and individual members performing renovation work shall obtain EPA RRP certification.** This is non-negotiable for Portland operations.

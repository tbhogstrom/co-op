## 4. Pre-Acquisition Cash Deployment Plan

Once capital contributions are collected, the co-op's cash must be allocated deliberately. Not every dollar is available for property acquisition. The following plan governs how collected capital flows into the co-op's account structure and what is available for the first flip.

### Account Structure

Per the capital structure memo (Section 6), the co-op will maintain three accounts:

| Account | Purpose | Initial Funding Source |
|---------|---------|----------------------|
| **Operating Checking** | Day-to-day overhead, formation costs, pre-acquisition expenses | Capital contributions (first tranche) |
| **Reserve Savings** | Operating reserve + contingency reserve; held separately, not to be commingled with operating funds | Capital contributions (locked at account opening) |
| **Project Trust** | Per-project funds during active flip (down payment, rehab draws, carrying costs) | Capital contributions (remainder after reserves and formation costs) |

### Cash Waterfall: From Collection to Deployment

The following waterfall applies as capital contributions are received. Each tier is funded before the next.

| Reserve | Amount | Basis |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Operating reserve (3 months overhead) | $14,600 | Capital structure memo, Section 5 |
| Project contingency reserve (15% of $60K rehab budget) | $9,000 | Minimum capitalization analysis, Guardrail #3 |
| **Total locked reserves** | **$23,600** | Transferred to Reserve Savings account immediately upon first contribution |

These funds are moved to the Reserve Savings account on the day Maven's contribution clears. They are not available for formation costs, acquisition equity, or any other purpose without a Board resolution per OA reserve fund provisions. This is Guardrail #3 from the minimum capitalization analysis: "Pre-acquisition reserve lockup: $23,600. Before closing on the first property, verify that $23,600 is set aside and untouchable."

### Deployment vs. Acquisition Requirements

Cross-referencing net deployable capital against the per-property equity requirements from the minimum capitalization analysis:

| Scenario | Net Deployable | $180K Property (needs $52,100) | $200K Property (needs $56,400 est.) | $220K Property (needs $59,700) |
|----------|---------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Conservative ($87K)** | $56,900 | $4,800 surplus | $500 surplus | ($2,800) deficit |
| **Moderate ($118.5K)** | $88,400 | $36,300 surplus | $32,000 surplus | $28,700 surplus |
| **Optimistic ($130K)** | $99,900 | $47,800 surplus | $43,500 surplus | $40,200 surplus |

### Key Findings

1. **Conservative scenario is razor-thin.** At $87K total capital, the co-op can barely fund a $180K acquisition and has zero margin at $200K+. This reinforces that the conservative case is a "do not proceed" scenario — we need at least the moderate outcome.

2. **Moderate scenario works within guardrails.** At $118.5K, the co-op has $88,400 deployable, which funds a $180K-$200K acquisition with a $32K-$36K surplus. This surplus absorbs a moderate rehab overrun (up to ~$25K) and covers 2-3 months of additional carrying costs if the project runs long. This is the sweet spot identified in the minimum capitalization analysis: a $180K-$200K house in Lents or Parkrose with a cosmetic rehab.

3. **Optimistic scenario adds breathing room but does not change the strategy.** At $130K, the surplus after a $180K acquisition is $47.8K. More comfortable, but still a single-flip-at-a-time operation. The extra cushion is welcome but does not unlock parallel projects (that requires ~$160K+ in deployable capital).

4. **The $200K target remains out of reach.** None of the scenarios approach $200K. The co-op should plan around the moderate case and treat the optimistic case as upside. The capital structure document's $200K target was aspirational; the minimum capitalization analysis already concluded that $115K is viable with guardrails.

5. **First-flip targeting is constrained.** Under the moderate scenario, Guardrail #1 (first acquisition ceiling: $200K purchase price) is financially binding, not just advisory. At $88.4K deployable, the math does not support a $220K purchase without accepting uncomfortably thin margins.

Capital collection will not happen all at once. The deployment timeline depends on the order contributions arrive:

| Date | Cumulative Capital | After Reserves + Formation | Can We Acquire? |

**Practical implication:** The co-op cannot close on a property until at least three founding members have contributed capital (Maven + two others). Realistically, this means earliest possible acquisition closing is late May. If the co-op wants the full moderate-scenario cushion before committing to a purchase, acquisition closing should target late June or early July — after Volt's contribution is received.

**Recommendation:** Begin property search now, identify candidates, conduct inspections, but do not make an offer until at least $80K in deployable capital is in the bank. This likely means waiting until Copper's contribution clears (mid-May at earliest). Make an offer only when the surplus after projected project equity need exceeds $20K.

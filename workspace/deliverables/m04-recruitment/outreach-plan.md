## Outreach Tiers and Tactics

### Tier 1: Direct Referral (Highest Yield — Do First)

**Time investment:** High per lead, but the conversion rate is dramatically higher than cold outreach.

**Harlan's network (primary)**  
20 years as a Portland residential GC means Harlan knows the electricians who show up, do it right, and can work as part of a team. My first move is sitting down with Harlan and getting 5 names of electricians he'd want on a rehab project. Same for HVAC if we go that direction.

*Action: Debrief with Harlan. Ask specifically: "Who's the most reliable licensed electrician you've put on a job in the past 3 years? Who do they remind you of when they're at their best?" Get names, not companies.*

**Birch, Slate, and Copper's networks**  
These three know Portland trades people. Once they commit to the co-op (pending vetting), I want to ask them: "Who's the electrician you'd want on a job with you?" Tradespeople know who's good. They work together on job sites.

*Action: After founding member interviews are complete, ask each one for 2 electrician referrals.*

**Union hall referrals**  
My contact at IBEW Local 48 (SW Portland hall) is a former union steward I know from my organizing days. I can ask — quietly, informally — whether there are journeyman members who are close to going out on their own and might be interested in a cooperative structure.

*Action: Call IBEW Local 48 contact. Explain what we're doing. Ask for informal referrals. Respect that this is a relationship, not a cold call.*

### Tier 2: Community Organizations (Relationship Outreach)

**Time investment:** Medium — requires showing up, not just calling.

**IBEW Local 48 — Direct**  
Beyond my personal contact, IBEW Local 48 has a member bulletin board and newsletter. Posting a co-op opportunity is appropriate — IBEW members are already union-minded and understand collective structures.

*What to post:* A short paragraph about the co-op. Not a pitch deck — just: "Worker-owned housing co-op forming in Portland. Seeking founding member with residential electrical license. Reach out to Calloway." A phone number. That's it.

*Timing: Post after founding member approvals are in progress — don't recruit before we can answer questions.*

**UA Local 290 — Plumbers & Steamfitters**  
Copper's union. Good relationship to cultivate for future members (plumbers, pipefitters). For Year 1, Copper covers this. For Year 2, if we want a second plumber or pipefitter, Local 290 is the first call.

**Pacific NW Regional Council of Carpenters**  
Birch's union. Same relationship logic. Year 1, Birch covers the carpentry need. Year 2, if we need a finish carpenter, a millworker, or a flooring specialist, PNWRCC is the source.

**Oregon Tradeswomen**  
Oregon Tradeswomen runs pre-apprenticeship programs and is deeply connected to women entering the trades in Oregon. They are not a source for founding members (too junior), but they are an important relationship to build for three reasons:

1. **Pipeline** — In 2–3 years, tradeswomen who came through their program will be reaching journeyman status. We want to be known in that community before we recruit from it.
2. **Values alignment** — A co-op that actively recruits tradeswomen and people of color in the trades sends a strong signal about who we are.
3. **Referrals** — Oregon Tradeswomen knows journeywomen who are further along. They may have referrals we can use now.

*Action: Contact Oregon Tradeswomen to introduce the co-op and ask about their journeywoman network. No immediate ask — relationship first.*

**Latino Network of Portland / Urban League of Portland**  
Portland's trades community includes many contractors of color operating as informal or under-licensed businesses. These individuals often have real skills and need the structure the co-op provides. This is a Year 2 focus — we need to have our first project under our belt to have credibility in this outreach.

*Action for now: Note this as a Year 2 priority. Reach out to both organizations to introduce the co-op after our first flip.*

### Tier 3: Contractor Networks (Targeted Prospecting)

**Time investment:** Medium — requires research, then outreach.

**Oregon CCB Licensee Database**  
The CCB database is public and searchable. I can filter by:
- Trade type: Electrical specialty, residential
- County: Multnomah, Washington, Clackamas
- License status: Active

This gives me a list of all active licensed residential electricians in the Portland metro. I can cross-reference against HomeAdvisor/Angi to find ones who have real project history and reviews.

*Use this for: Finding the 4th and 5th founding member candidates if direct referrals don't surface the right people within 3 weeks.*

**HomeAdvisor / Angi profiles**  
Not glamorous, but useful for finding sole proprietors with an active project history. I look for:
- Licensed (CCB # visible)
- 10+ reviews with consistent quality signals
- Residential specialty (not commercial)
- Based in NE/SE/N Portland (likely to know our target neighborhoods)

**Portland neighborhood contractor Facebook groups**  
- "Portland Homeowners and Contractors" (12K+ members)
- "PDX Trades Network"
- "Northeast Portland Neighbors" — homeowner group, but contractors lurk

These are useful for listening and for posting a brief intro to the co-op. Not my preferred channel, but can surface people I wouldn't otherwise find.

### Tier 4: Events and Spaces (Community Presence)

**Time investment:** Medium-low per event, but cumulative relationship-building.

**Portland Building Trades Council events**  
The Building Trades Council convenes union trades people from across the city. Annual events, organizing meetings, political engagement. I know people in this world from my union organizing days. Showing up as the Portland Housing Co-op recruiter puts us in front of the right people.

*Relevant events in the next 90 days: May Day labor celebration, Portland Building Trades annual meeting (May/June).*

**Portland Housing Bureau community events**  
The PHB is actively engaged in affordable housing and community development. A co-op that rehabs distressed homes in Lents and Cully fits their mission. Connecting with PHB staff could:
- Provide us with credibility references for potential members
- Potentially surface property leads (bonus)
- Open doors to funding or partnership programs for worker co-ops

*Action: Request a meeting with PHB to introduce the co-op concept. No immediate ask — just relationship.*

**Neighborhood association meetings (Lents, Cully, Parkrose)**  
These are the neighborhoods we'll work in. Attending the neighborhood association meetings serves two purposes:
1. Community relationships — We're not an extractive flipper; we're a community-rooted co-op
2. Referrals — Neighbors know local contractors. A trusted voice in the room can produce the best referrals.

*Action: Attend Lents, Cully, and Parkrose NA meetings within 60 days of co-op formation.*

## Outreach Message Framework

### For direct referral conversations (phone / in person)

> "I'm building a worker-owned housing co-op in Portland. We buy distressed homes in Lents, Cully, and Parkrose, rehab them with a skilled founding crew, and split the profit based on labor hours and capital contributed. We're looking for a licensed electrician who wants to be a co-owner, not just a sub. Know anybody like that?"

Short. Direct. Lets them pattern-match quickly.

### For union hall posting

> **Worker-Owned Housing Co-op — Portland**
>
> The Portland Housing Co-op is forming a founding crew of licensed tradespeople who want to own the projects they work on — not just work them.
>
> We're looking for a licensed residential electrician for our founding member cohort. Profit-sharing model. One member, one vote. Serious about quality.
>
> This is for experienced journeymen who are ready for more than a day rate.
>
> Call Calloway: [phone]

### For community events (brief verbal intro)

> "We're starting a housing co-op in Portland — tradespeople as member-owners. We buy houses in Lents and Cully, fix them right with a founding crew, and split the profit. Looking for a licensed electrician who's ready to co-own something. Know anyone?"

Three sentences. Plant the seed. Let them come back to me.

## DEI Commitment

The Portland Housing Co-op is committed to building a founding cohort that reflects Portland's trades community — including tradespeople of color, women in trades, and workers who haven't historically had access to ownership in the housing sector.

Practical commitments in Year 1:
- Actively seek referrals from Oregon Tradeswomen and Portland's Latino trades community
- Do not limit outreach to union members only (many skilled tradespeople of color are non-union)
- Apply the same vetting criteria consistently to all candidates — no lower standards, no favoritism
- Communicate the co-op's commitment to equity explicitly in outreach materials

This is not a quota system. It is an active commitment to look beyond the default network and give every qualified tradesperson a real shot at founding membership.

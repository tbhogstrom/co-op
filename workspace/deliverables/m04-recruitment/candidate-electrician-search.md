## 1. Why Electrician Is the Critical Gap

Every Portland residential rehab touches electrical. There is no exception. Here is the reality of what the co-op will face on every project:

**Knob-and-tube wiring.** Portland's pre-1940s housing stock is full of it. Lents, Cully, and Parkrose all have significant concentrations of 1920s-1940s homes with original knob-and-tube. Insurance companies increasingly refuse to cover homes with active K&T. Any flip that leaves K&T in the walls is a flip that limits the buyer pool and depresses the sale price. A licensed electrician who can identify, document, and remediate K&T is not optional — it is foundational.

**Panel upgrades.** Most pre-1960s Portland homes have 60A or 100A service panels. Modern buyer expectations (EV charging, heat pumps, kitchen appliances) require 200A service. A panel upgrade is a $3,000-$5,000 scope item on nearly every project. Without a member electrician, we subcontract this at full retail plus GC markup — the exact economics the co-op exists to invert.

**Aluminum wiring.** Portland's 1960s-1970s housing stock (ranches in outer Lents, Parkrose) frequently has aluminum branch wiring. This is a safety hazard and an inspection flag. Remediation requires a licensed electrician.

**Code compliance.** Oregon Electrical Specialty Code requires a licensed electrician for virtually all electrical work beyond basic fixture swaps. The co-op cannot self-perform electrical scope without a licensed member.

**Cost of subcontracting.** Electrical subcontracting on a typical Portland rehab runs $8,000-$20,000 depending on scope. If we run 2-3 flips per year, that is $16,000-$60,000 in labor value leaving the co-op annually. Bringing that in-house through a member electrician captures that value for the membership.

**Bottom line:** Without an electrician, the co-op operates with a structural dependency on subcontractors for the single most code-intensive and liability-sensitive trade in residential rehab. That is not sustainable.

- Establish a preferred subcontractor relationship with a licensed electrician (ideally Volt or Wren even if they decline membership)
- Negotiate a fixed-rate agreement for common scopes (panel upgrade, re-wire per room, K&T remediation)
- Continue recruiting for an electrician member during Year 1 with the goal of bringing one in before the second flip

### Backup C: Modify the founding cohort target

If electrician recruitment proves impossible within 10 weeks, consider:
- Filling the 5th founding slot with a different high-value trade (HVAC mechanic, tile setter, or drywall specialist)
- Keeping the 6th slot explicitly reserved for an electrician, to be filled as a "Year 1 addition" rather than founding member
- Adjusting capitalization targets accordingly

**My strong preference is Backup A before B, and B before C.** An electrician on the founding team is the single highest-value recruitment win remaining. I will exhaust the search before accepting subcontractor dependency.

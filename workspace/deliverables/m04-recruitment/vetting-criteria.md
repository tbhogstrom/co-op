# Vetting Criteria & Scoring Rubric — Portland Housing Co-op

**Version:** 1.0  
**Author:** Calloway  
**Date:** 2026-04-08  
**Purpose:** Standardized vetting process for all membership applicants. Prevents favoritism, ensures compliance, and documents the basis for membership decisions.

---

## Vetting Philosophy

We're not just checking boxes — we're choosing co-owners. A member who passes every technical requirement but undermines the co-op culture costs us more than a skills gap we can sub out.

That said, the technical requirements are non-negotiable. No license = no membership. No EPA RRP = no project participation. These are bright lines, not suggestions.

The vetting process has three stages:

1. **Compliance verification** — Pass/fail. No exceptions.
2. **Scored assessment** — 100-point rubric covering skills, experience, and culture fit.
3. **Final interview** — Calloway + Maven. Holistic gut-check.

A candidate must pass Stage 1 before Stage 2 scores matter. Stage 3 is the human layer that no rubric can replace.

## Stage 1: Compliance Verification (Pass / Fail)

*All items must be confirmed before proceeding to scoring. Failure on any item is disqualifying.*

### 1.1 CCB License Verification

**How:** Oregon CCB online database — [https://www.oregon.gov/ccb](https://www.oregon.gov/ccb)

| Check | Pass Criteria | Fail Criteria |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| License active | Status: Active | Expired, suspended, revoked |
| License matches trade | Endorsement matches claimed trade | Wrong endorsement category |
| CCB complaints | Zero active complaints | Any active complaint or unresolved dispute |
| Disciplinary actions | Clean record | Any civil penalty, suspension, or revocation in past 5 years |
| RMI eligibility | Verified | If being considered as RMI for entity license |

**Verification notes to document:**  
- Date verified  
- License # confirmed  
- Expiration date  
- Any complaints found (including resolved)  
- Verifier (Calloway)

### 1.2 Insurance Verification

**How:** Request ACORD certificate of insurance directly from applicant's insurer, naming co-op as certificate holder.

| Coverage | Minimum Required | Pass Criteria |
|----------|-----------------|---------------|
| General Liability — per occurrence | $500,000 | Policy active, limit meets minimum |
| General Liability — aggregate | $1,000,000 | Policy active, limit meets minimum |
| Policy expiration | Must be current | Not expired at time of application |
| Workers' Comp | Required if applicant has employees | Active, or confirmed sole operator exempt |

### 1.3 Surety Bond Verification

| Check | Pass Criteria |
|-------|--------------|
| Bond active | Confirmed active by bonding company |
| Bond amount | Meets CCB minimums ($20,000 residential) |
| Bond expiration | Current at time of application |

### 1.4 EPA RRP Certification

| Check | Pass Criteria | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| EPA RRP certification | Active certification | Check epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program |
| Certification current | Not expired | 5-year renewal cycle |

*If not certified: Document clearly. This is a hard requirement for project participation. Member may join pending certification if they commit to completing it within 60 days. No exceptions after that window.*

### 1.5 Specialty Trade License (where applicable)

| Trade | Required License | Verification Source |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Plumbing | Oregon BCD Journeyman or Master Plumber | Oregon BCD license lookup |
| Electrical | Oregon Electrical license | Oregon BCD license lookup |
| HVAC | Oregon limited energy or mechanical license | Oregon BCD license lookup |

## Stage 2: Scored Assessment (100 Points)

### 2.1 Technical Skills (35 points)

*Assessed through the skills inventory in the application, job references, and site conversation. Not a formal test.*

#### Depth in Primary Trade (20 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **18–20** | Expert: 10+ years in primary trade; can lead independently, handle unusual conditions, and troubleshoot without guidance; has done specialty work in Portland's older housing stock |
| **14–17** | Proficient: 7–10 years in primary trade; handles standard conditions independently; knows Portland's era-specific issues |
| **10–13** | Competent: 5–7 years; can execute standard scope; learning curve on some conditions |
| **6–9** | Developing: 3–5 years; needs oversight on complex work |
| **0–5** | Insufficient for founding membership |

*Birch benchmark: 19/20 (structural assessment and period-appropriate finish work)*  
*Slate benchmark: 18/20 (composition and metal roofing, PNW-specific knowledge)*  
*Copper benchmark: 20/20 (18 years Portland residential, 10 focused on rehab)*

#### Breadth / Trade Coordination (10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **9–10** | Has regularly coordinated with at least 2 other trades; understands sequencing (e.g., rough-in before drywall); flags conditions for other trades proactively |
| **7–8** | Has worked on multi-trade projects; understands the other trades' needs at a general level |
| **5–6** | Mostly solo or single-trade; limited coordination experience |
| **0–4** | No significant multi-trade experience |

#### Portland Housing Stock Knowledge (5 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **5** | Can walk into a pre-1960 Portland home and articulate the likely problems for their trade before opening walls; knows common conditions by era |
| **4** | Strong knowledge of at least 2 housing eras common in target neighborhoods |
| **3** | General knowledge; learns quickly on site |
| **0–2** | Limited Portland-specific experience |

---

### 2.2 Licensing, Credentials & Compliance (15 points)

*All Stage 1 requirements must be met. This section scores quality of compliance — not just "yes or no" but how robust.*

| Dimension | Points | Criteria |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| CCB license tenure | 0–5 | 5 = 5+ years active license with clean record; 3 = 2–5 years clean; 1 = under 2 years |
| EPA RRP + additional certs | 0–5 | 5 = EPA RRP current + OSHA 30 + at least one specialty certification; 3 = EPA RRP + OSHA 10; 1 = EPA RRP only |
| Insurance / bond quality | 0–5 | 5 = exceeds minimums, long-term relationship with carrier; 3 = meets minimums; 1 = meets minimums, recent policy |

---

### 2.3 Financial Capacity & Stability (15 points)

*We are not demanding financial disclosure beyond what the application asks. This score is based on evidence of business stability, not precise income.*

| Dimension | Points | Criteria |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Business duration | 0–5 | 5 = 5+ years in business; 3 = 2–5 years; 1 = under 2 years |
| Buy-in tier capacity | 0–5 | 5 = Tier A ($25K+) without hardship; 4 = Tier B ($10K–25K); 3 = Tier C ($5K) comfortable; 1 = Tier C with some difficulty |
| Financial systems | 0–5 | 5 = separate business accounts, files business taxes separately, prior experience with business partnership; 3 = basic separation; 1 = informal |

---

### 2.4 Culture Fit & Co-op Alignment (25 points)

*Assessed from the application's open-ended questions, references, and interview. This is the section that requires the most judgment.*

#### Collaborative Orientation (10 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **9–10** | References and track record consistently show this person communicates proactively, flags issues early for the team, and has a history of long-term professional relationships (recurring GC clients, union involvement, stable partnerships) |
| **7–8** | Generally collaborative; some evidence of team-orientation; references describe as reliable partner |
| **5–6** | Neutral — works solo by preference but can collaborate; no negative signals |
| **3–4** | Red flags — "lone wolf" patterns, history of short-term relationships, references describe communication problems |
| **0–2** | Clear misalignment with collaborative culture |

#### Democratic Process Acceptance (8 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **8** | Application language and interview shows genuine comfort with collective decision-making; has experience in shared governance (union, co-op, board, partnership) |
| **6–7** | Understands the concept; willing to engage; no red flags about control needs |
| **4–5** | Some hesitancy; may need time to adjust; not a deal-breaker |
| **2–3** | Significant concern — language suggests difficulty giving up unilateral control |
| **0–1** | Clear incompatibility ("I make my own calls, period") |

#### Quality Commitment (7 points)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **7** | Unprompted references to quality standards in application; references describe them as the person who won't cut corners; has turned down work over quality concerns |
| **5–6** | Quality-focused when asked; no evidence of corner-cutting in history |
| **4** | Neutral — average quality orientation |
| **0–3** | Red flags — references describe complaints, call-backs, or shortcuts |

---

### 2.5 References (10 points)

*We speak to all three references. Score is based on the quality and substance of those conversations.*

| Dimension | Points | Criteria |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Reference quality | 0–4 | 4 = All 3 are substantive professional references who worked with candidate recently; 2 = Mixed quality; 0 = Weak, unavailable, or missing references |
| Specific endorsement of skills | 0–3 | 3 = References give specific examples of excellent work; 1 = General positive comments without specifics |
| Character / culture endorsement | 0–3 | 3 = References speak specifically to reliability, communication, and integrity; 1 = "Great person" without substance |

| Score | Recommendation |
|-------|---------------|

## Stage 3: Final Interview

**Participants:** Calloway + Maven (at minimum). If other founding members are already committed, invite one to observe.

**Format:** Approximately 90 minutes, conversational. Review their application together. Let them lead the conversation about their work.

**Key objectives:**
1. Verify that the person matches the application (no false impressions)
2. Get their honest read on what they're hoping for and worried about
3. Assess interpersonal dynamics — would you want to be in a tense budget meeting with this person?
4. Walk through the financial model and membership agreement in plain language

### Required Topics in Interview

| Topic | What we're listening for |
|-------|------------------------|
| Their best work story | Pride in craft, attention to detail, problem-solving |
| A project that went wrong | Honesty, accountability, lessons learned |
| How they've handled a disagreement | Emotional regulation, communication style |
| What they know about co-ops | Genuine curiosity vs. just wanting the money |
| Their concerns about membership | Thoughtful concerns > no concerns (no concerns means they didn't think hard) |
| Their timeline and availability | Practical alignment with our project schedule |
| The EPA RRP requirement | Must confirm they understand and will comply |
| The buy-in commitment | Must confirm this is affordable without hardship |

### Interview Red Flags

Stop the process and escalate to Maven if any of the following appear in the interview:

- Dismissal of the "one member, one vote" structure ("Yeah but in practice you'd still decide, right?")
- "I don't really do the paperwork" in response to questions about licensing or permits
- Hostility toward their previous GCs or clients without accountability
- "I just want to know I'll get paid fairly" without any expressed interest in the cooperative model
- Pressure to skip reference checks ("You don't need to call them, I can tell you what they'll say")
- Reluctance to discuss the EPA RRP requirement or any compliance topic

## Reference Check Protocol

**Call all three references.** Do not accept email responses for the reference check. Phone is required — tone matters.

**Questions to ask every reference:**
1. How long have you worked with [candidate] and in what capacity?
2. Can you describe a specific project where their work was especially strong?
3. Have you ever seen them in a situation where the scope changed unexpectedly? How did they handle it?
4. How do they communicate when there's a problem on a job?
5. Would you hire them again? Any hesitation?
6. Is there anything about working with them that took adjustment?

**Trade-specific add-ons:**

For carpentry references: "Did you ever see [Birch] flag a structural issue that others had missed?"

For roofing references: "How does [Slate] respond when a project runs into unexpected deck conditions?"

For plumbing references: "Has [Copper] ever pushed back on a scope that he thought was being underpriced or cut short?"

**Document all reference calls:**  
Date / time / duration / who spoke / summary of responses / any red flags noted.

## 10. Red Flags or Concerns

1. **Healthy skepticism, not yet fully committed.** Slate is asking the right questions but has not yet said "I'm in." He wants to see the operating agreement language on exit/buyout (from Statton) and wants to meet the other founding members before deciding. This is appropriate due diligence, not cold feet. Next step: get him in front of Birch and Copper. The interpersonal chemistry will close him.

2. **Solo operator for 7 years.** Slate is accustomed to making his own calls on scheduling, materials, and scope. Democratic co-op governance will require adjustment. He acknowledged this: "I know I'll have to compromise on things I'm used to deciding alone." The self-awareness is the good sign.

3. **CCB license expires 2026-12-31.** Routine renewal expected, but must be tracked. If co-op projects begin in months 4-5 (August-September 2026), his license will be active. Must confirm renewal by November 2026.

4. **Weather-dependent scheduling.** Roofing in Portland means working around rain. October through March is challenging. Harlan will need to build weather float into any project schedule involving Slate's roofing scope. This is a trade reality, not a candidate deficiency.

## What We Are

The Portland Housing Cooperative is a **worker-owned cooperative LLC** forming in Portland, Oregon. We buy distressed residential properties, rehabilitate them using member labor, and sell them at full market value. Profit flows to the people who did the work.

We are not a staffing agency. We are not a GC with a profit-sharing plan. We are a cooperative — every member is an owner, every owner has one vote, and the economics are structured so that skilled tradespeople capture the value their labor creates.

**Entity type:** Cooperative LLC (Oregon)  
**Governance:** One member, one vote — regardless of capital contribution  
**Target neighborhoods:** Lents, Cully, Parkrose (Portland east side)  
**Target properties:** Distressed single-family homes, purchase price $180K-$280K, ARV $350K-$450K  
**Founding team:** 6 members — licensed tradespeople and a project coordinator

## How It Works

### The Five-Phase Cycle

```
SOURCE --> ACQUIRE --> REHAB --> SELL --> DISTRIBUTE
```

**1. Source** — Our real estate analyst identifies distressed properties in target neighborhoods. We run conservative numbers before making any offer. If the ROI is not there, we walk.

**2. Acquire** — Purchase using a combination of member equity and hard money financing (85% LTV). Clean title, clear terms.

**3. Rehab** — Members execute the scope of work. All hours are logged daily by trade type and task. The log is the ledger — your hours determine your labor share.

**4. Sell** — List at after-repair value (ARV). Professional staging. Pre-defined price reduction triggers. We do not get emotional about a number.

**5. Distribute** — Within 2 weeks of sale close, profit is split according to the formula below. Checks go out. Equity accounts are updated.

**Target timeline per flip:** 5-9 months (acquisition through sale)  
**Target flips per year:** 2-3

## Financial Summary

### Buy-In Tiers

Every founding member contributes capital to the co-op. This is equity — you own a share of the enterprise, not a fee you pay.

| Tier | Amount | What It Means |
|------|--------|---------------|
| **Tier A — Full Share** | $25,000 - $50,000 | Larger capital share of profits. Same one vote as everyone else. |
| **Tier B — Working Share** | $10,000 - $24,999 | Most common founding tier. Balances capital return with labor return. |
| **Tier C — Sweat Equity Entry** | $5,000 | Minimum buy-in. Your wealth builds primarily through labor share. Still one full vote. |

**Important:** Your buy-in tier does NOT affect your voting power. One member, one vote. Period. A Tier C member has the same governance authority as a Tier A member. Your buy-in determines your share of the capital distribution — your labor hours determine your share of the labor distribution.

**No payment plans for founding members.** Capital contribution is due in full before the first project begins.

### Profit Split Formula

When a property sells and all costs are paid, the net profit is distributed as follows:

| Category | Share | How It Works |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| **Labor** | 40% | Divided among members proportional to their weighted hours logged on the project. Trade rate weights range from 1.0x (general labor) to 1.3x (GC/project management). Your trade, your hours, your share. |
| **Capital** | 30% | Divided among members proportional to their capital account balance (buy-in amount). |
| **Reserves** | 20% | Retained by the co-op for future acquisitions, operating expenses, and contingency. Builds the co-op's capacity for the next project. |
| **Overhead** | 10% | Covers insurance, accounting, legal, and administrative costs. |

### Example: What a Member Might Earn

*Conservative scenario — single flip, $40,750 net profit:*

| Member | Buy-in | Capital Share (30%) | Hours | Weight | Labor Share (40%) | Total Distribution |
|--------|--------|--------------------:|-------|--------|------------------:|-------------------:|
| Carpenter (Tier B, $15K) | $15,000 | ~$917 | 250 hrs | 1.1x | ~$1,860 | **~$2,777** |
| Plumber (Tier B, $25K) | $25,000 | ~$1,528 | 350 hrs | 1.2x | ~$3,423 | **~$4,951** |
| Roofer (Tier C, $5K) | $5,000 | ~$306 | 100 hrs | 1.1x | ~$897 | **~$1,203** |

*These are conservative estimates based on a single flip. With 2-3 flips per year, annual returns scale accordingly. Your capital stays in the co-op (growing your capital account) — it is not consumed by the first project.*

### Labor Advances

During a rehab project, you may need cash before the property sells. The co-op offers **labor advances** of up to 50% of your estimated conservative labor share. This is an advance against future distribution, not a loan. It comes out of your final distribution when the property sells.

**Example:** If your estimated labor share on a project is $3,000, you can draw up to $1,500 during the rehab phase.

## What Membership Means

### Your Rights as a Member

- **One vote** on all major decisions: project selection, budget approval, new member admission, profit distribution, amendments to the operating agreement
- **Access to co-op projects** — priority work for members over subcontractors
- **Capital account** — your buy-in is tracked and grows with retained capital distributions
- **Labor share** — every hour you work on a co-op project earns toward your distribution
- **Labor advances** — up to 50% of estimated conservative labor share during active projects
- **Exit and buyout** — if you leave the co-op, your capital account balance is returned per the operating agreement schedule (structured over 12-24 months to protect co-op cash flow)
- **Transparency** — full access to financial records, project budgets, and co-op accounts

### Your Obligations as a Member

- **Capital contribution** — pay your buy-in before the first project
- **Active participation** — this is not a passive investment. Members work on projects. If you are not logging hours, you are not earning labor share.
- **Licensing and insurance** — maintain your Oregon CCB license, trade licenses, general liability insurance, and EPA RRP certification at all times. A lapsed license is a compliance problem for the entire co-op.
- **Governance participation** — attend monthly member meetings. Vote on decisions. Serve on committees as needed. Democracy requires showing up.
- **Quality standards** — the co-op's reputation depends on every member's work. We do not cut corners. If you are not comfortable with that standard, this is not the right fit.
- **Transparency** — log your hours honestly. Communicate schedule changes. Flag problems early.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can I keep my existing business and clients while being a co-op member?

**Yes.** Founding members are structured as independent contractor-members, not employees. You maintain your own CCB license, your own insurance, and your own client relationships. Co-op project work is in addition to your existing business. During an active rehab (typically 3-6 months), expect to dedicate 15-30 hours per week to co-op work depending on your trade scope and the project phase. You manage your own schedule.

### 2. What if we lose money on a flip?

Losses are shared proportionally through the capital accounts. Your personal liability is limited to your capital contribution — you do not owe additional money beyond your buy-in. The operating agreement's 20% reserve allocation is specifically designed to buffer against losses and protect the co-op's ability to continue operations after a bad project.

### 3. What happens if I want to leave the co-op?

The operating agreement provides for a structured buyout of your capital account balance. Buyout is paid over 12-24 months to protect co-op cash flow. You receive your capital account balance (original buy-in plus retained capital distributions minus any losses). You do not receive retroactive labor share for projects completed after your departure.

### 4. How are disputes resolved?

The operating agreement includes a three-step dispute resolution process: (1) direct conversation between the parties, (2) mediation by a neutral member or outside mediator, (3) binding arbitration if mediation fails. For day-to-day project decisions, the GC/project manager has operational authority within the approved budget and scope. For decisions outside that scope, the membership votes.

### 5. I am not a union member. Can I still join?

**Yes.** Union membership is preferred (it signals quality training and familiarity with collective structures) but is not required. What is required: active Oregon CCB license, appropriate trade license (for plumbing, electrical), GL insurance, EPA RRP certification, and a track record of quality work on Portland residential projects.

### 6. What trades are you looking for right now?

Our highest priority is a **licensed electrician** for the founding cohort. We are also open to conversations with HVAC mechanics, drywall/finish specialists, and tile setters for the 6th founding slot or future membership.

### 7. How much time do meetings and governance take?

Expect one monthly member meeting (2 hours) plus occasional committee or project planning sessions. During active rehab, there will be weekly project check-ins (30 minutes). Total governance time: approximately 5-8 hours per month. This is the cost of democratic ownership — and it is worth it.

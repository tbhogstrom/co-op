## Slate — Roofer (Score: 84/100)

**Status: IN VETTING — Cautiously advancing**

### Terms Summary Response

Slate received the terms summary (slate-terms-summary.md) on April 9. I followed up by phone on April 15. He had read it. All of it. He had three questions:

**Question 1: "If the co-op can pay my capital back over 12 months instead of 60 days, what triggers that?"**

I explained that the board makes that determination based on cash position. If paying a lump sum would drop the co-op below $20,000 in operating cash, the installment option activates. Slate said: "So if the co-op is doing well, I get it in 60 days. If it's tight, it takes a year." Correct. He did not love the 12-month scenario but accepted that it is a protection for everyone, including him. He said: "I'd want to know what the cash position is before I give notice."

I told him that as a member he would have full visibility into financials at every monthly meeting. That satisfied him.

**Question 2: "The non-compete — 12 months. That was 6 months before. Why did it change?"**

I explained that Statton recommended the extension to 12 months during the operating agreement amendment process because a 6-month restriction was likely unenforceable under Oregon law for this type of covenant. The 12-month version is narrowly tailored and defensible. Slate was slightly unhappy about the increase but said: "If it's just flipping and just Portland, I can live with 12 months. I'm not planning to flip houses on my own anyway."

**Question 3: "When do I get to meet the other people?"**

This is the question I was waiting for. Slate has been deliberate and document-focused throughout the process. Asking to meet the cohort means he has moved past the paper stage and wants to evaluate the people. I told him about the April 29 founders' meeting and that he is invited. He said: "I'll be there."

### Tracking Toward April 29 Meeting

Slate is confirmed for April 29. He wants to see the final operating agreement before that meeting but understands it may not be ready in time (Statton's target is week of April 22). I told him the current draft covers all the terms in the summary, and the amendments are refinements rather than structural changes. He accepted that.

**Assessment:** Slate is advancing. The terms summary did its job — it gave him the information he needed, and his questions were sharp but not adversarial. The fact that he asked to meet the cohort is a significant step forward. Slate's decision will likely come down to his impression of Birch, Copper, and Maven at the founders' meeting. If the chemistry works, he is in. If the people do not feel right to him, no amount of favorable terms will close him.

**Risk factor remains:** Slate is the most likely candidate to walk if something feels off. He is careful, and careful people need to be respected, not rushed.

## 4. Experience Summary

**15 years total**, starting as a laborer on roofing crews at 19, worked up through tear-off, installation, and eventually lead installer before going independent 7 years ago.

**Project history (representative):**
- 5 years on crew with Pacific NW Roofing Solutions (regional residential/light commercial roofer)
- 3 years as lead installer for Cascade Roofing (residential, focused on Portland metro)
- 7 years self-employed (Slate Roofing Co.), 20-35 residential projects per year

**Specializations:**
- Composition shingle tear-off and re-roof (primary bread and butter)
- Standing seam metal roofing (increasingly popular in Portland for longevity)
- Low-slope / flat systems: TPO and modified bitumen (garages, additions, flat-top sections common on Portland Craftsmans)
- Flashing and waterproofing — knows how Portland's weather tests every penetration
- Ventilation design — ridge/soffit/gable vent systems, attic fans
- Gutter systems — installation and drainage planning
- Moss treatment and prevention (zinc strips, copper flashing — PNW essential knowledge)
- Skylight installation and replacement

**Portland-specific expertise:**
Slate knows Portland roofing cold. He knows that a house in the West Hills needs different drainage planning than a flat-lot bungalow in Sellwood. He knows which neighborhoods have trees that destroy gutters and which have moss that will eat a roof in seven years without zinc. He estimates by the square and he knows the labor math for every system he installs.

## 7. Culture Fit Assessment

**Strengths:**
- Honest about scope: he has a strong track record of flagging additional conditions (deck rot, improper ventilation) before they become change-order surprises. Co-ops hate surprises.
- Cost-principled: deeply opposed to cheap roofing that fails in 5 years. The co-op model rewards long-term thinking — Slate naturally operates that way.
- Independent operator mindset: has run his own business for 7 years, so he understands revenue, costs, and the pain of a bad job. He'll engage with co-op financials, not just tune them out.
- Community roots: St. Johns is a working-class neighborhood with a strong sense of community. Slate is embedded in that network.
- Willing to work with others: coordinates regularly with general contractors and other trades on multi-trade jobs. Not a lone wolf.

**Potential friction points:**
- Can be rigid about his "right way" — specifically on roofing specs. Will push back hard if the co-op tries to value-engineer a roof below his standards. This is largely a virtue, but needs a clear dispute resolution process.
- Solo-operator for 7 years means he's not used to collective decision-making. Democratic processes will be a learning curve.
- Weather-dependent scheduling means his availability during a project can be unpredictable. Harlan will need to build weather float into any schedule involving Slate's work.

**My read:** Slate's the most skeptical of the three candidates about the co-op concept — he'll ask hard questions about liability, buy-in, and "what happens when we disagree." That's actually a sign he's serious, not a sign he's not interested. I want to get him in front of Birch and Copper early. I think the interpersonal chemistry will close him more than the financial pitch.

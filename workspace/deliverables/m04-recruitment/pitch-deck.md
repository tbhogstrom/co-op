<!-- SLIDE 3: THERE IS ANOTHER WAY -->

## What if you were the developer?

**The Portland Housing Co-op** is a worker-owned LLC that buys distressed homes, rehabilitates them using member labor, and sells them at full market value.

The difference:

- **Members are owner-operators, not subcontractors.**
- **Profit is split among the people who did the work** — by capital contributed and hours logged.
- **One member, one vote.** No exceptions. No silent partners. No founder veto.

We are not a staffing agency. We are not a GC with a loyalty program.  
We are a cooperative, and the economics flow accordingly.

<!-- SLIDE 4: HOW A FLIP WORKS -->

## The five-phase cycle.

```
SOURCE → ACQUIRE → REHAB → SELL → DISTRIBUTE
```

**1. Source** *(2–4 weeks)*  
Reeves (our real estate analyst) identifies distressed properties in Lents, Cully, and Parkrose. We run numbers before we make any offer. Rule: never offer on a deal where conservative ROI < 15%. We walk away before we stretch.

**2. Acquire** *(2–4 weeks)*  
Purchase with a combination of member equity and hard money financing (85% LTV). Statton reviews title. Ledger locks loan terms. We close clean.

**3. Rehab** *(3–6 months)*  
Members execute the scope of work planned by Harlan (our GC). All hours are logged daily — trade type, task, hours. The log is the ledger. No hours = no labor share.

**4. Sell** *(1–3 months)*  
List at ARV. Professional staging and photography. Pre-defined price reduction triggers — we don't get emotional about a number.

**5. Distribute** *(within 2 weeks of sale close)*  
Ledger runs the profit-split formula. Checks go out. Equity accounts are updated.

## Non-negotiables — for the co-op and for you.

### Licensing & compliance (required for membership — no exceptions)

- ✅ **Active Oregon CCB license** with applicable endorsement
- ✅ **General liability insurance** — minimum $500,000/$1,000,000
- ✅ **Surety bond** (per CCB requirements for your license type)
- ✅ **EPA RRP certification** — mandatory for all members
  - *Portland's housing stock is predominantly pre-1978. Every project involves lead paint risk. No cert = no work on co-op projects.*
- ✅ **Clean CCB record** — no active complaints, no disciplinary actions

### Governance (the democratic floor)

- **One member, one vote** — on project selection, new member admission, major capital decisions. No founder has a veto. No big investor has extra votes.
- **2/3 supermajority** for property acquisition and capital decisions over $50K.
- **Transparent books** — Ledger's reports are available to all members at all times.
- **Disputes go to the member meeting** — not to one person's desk.

### Quality standards

We do not do cheap flips. We do not hide conditions behind fresh paint.  
We rehab to code, we use proper materials, and we stand behind our work after the sale.  
This protects our reputation, our licenses, and the next buyer.

<!-- SLIDE 11: THE HONEST PART -->

## What we're not telling you — but should.

**The timeline is real.** From the day you write your buy-in check to the day you receive your first distribution: **12–14 months**. That's how long it takes to form, capitalize, find the right property, execute the rehab, and close the sale. We will not speed-run this.

**The first flip will be harder than it looks.** We're a new team. Coordination takes time. There will be days when the schedule slips and the budget gets tight. That's construction. The 15% contingency exists for a reason.

**Democratic decisions are sometimes slower.** A vote can go against you. That's the model. If you need unilateral control of a job, a co-op isn't it.

**The labor tracking is real.** Your labor share is based on logged hours. If you don't log your hours, you lose that share. We take the ledger seriously.

**Your buy-in is at risk.** This is a real estate investment. If a project loses money, member capital accounts may be reduced. The 20% reserve fund and the ≤65%-of-ARV purchase rule exist to protect against this. But risk is real.

**EPA RRP certification is mandatory.** If you don't have it, you'll need to get it before your first co-op project. This is not negotiable. Cost is approximately $200–$300 and a one-day course.

<!-- SLIDE 12: YOUR QUESTIONS -->

## The questions we know you have.

**Q: What if the project loses money?**  
A: Losses first reduce the reserve fund, then are distributed proportionally to capital accounts. Your personal liability is limited to your buy-in equity. You will not receive a bill.

**Q: What if I need to leave?**  
A: Orderly exit process per the Operating Agreement. Your capital account is redeemable at book value with 90-day notice. No withdrawal during an active project. Your equity goes with you.

**Q: What about my existing work?**  
A: Co-op membership is not exclusive. You keep your own clients and your own business. Co-op projects are an addition, not a replacement.

**Q: Who decides which properties we buy?**  
A: Reeves presents the deal. Ledger runs the numbers. All members vote. 2/3 supermajority required to proceed. If the numbers don't work, any member can say no.

**Q: What if I disagree with the direction we're taking?**  
A: Your vote counts. The dispute resolution process in the Operating Agreement covers persistent disagreements. And if it ever truly doesn't work — there's an exit.

**Q: What are the tax implications?**  
A: Distributions are taxable income. The co-op will issue K-1s. Statton and Ledger will brief all founding members on the tax picture before they commit. You should talk to your own accountant.

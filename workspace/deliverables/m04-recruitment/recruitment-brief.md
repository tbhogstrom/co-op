## What We're Solving For

Portland tradespeople are not hard to find. Skilled, licensed, financially stable ones who *also* want to be co-op members — that's the harder cut.

The pitch has two audiences with different fears:

**Audience A — The Independent Contractor**  
They already have their own work. They're skeptical of "partnerships" that feel like unpaid meetings and shared liability for someone else's mistakes. They need to see a clear financial upside, a sensible governance structure, and proof that the co-op respects their time.

**Audience B — The Union Member (journeyman-era, maybe 10+ years in)**  
They have stable wages but limited upside. They've seen the economics of the houses they build and watched owners cash out while they walk away with a day rate. They're primed for the co-op pitch — they understand collective bargaining, shared risk, and solidarity — but they worry about legal exposure and capital requirements.

Our three initial candidates (Birch, Slate, Copper) likely straddle both.

## The Core Pitch: Why a Housing Co-op?

### Problem we're solving *for tradespeople*

Portland's real estate market has made skilled tradespeople economically invisible. A carpenter in SE Portland earns $45-65/hr. The GC they work for earns a 20-35% markup on that. The developer earns the upside on the asset. The tradesperson provides the skilled labor that creates the value — and walks away with a wage.

A housing co-op inverts that. The co-op *is* the developer. Members are the owners. Profit flows to labor, not capital.

### What co-op membership offers (preliminary — pending M3 financial model)

| Benefit | Traditional Subcontracting | Co-op Membership |
|---------|--------------------------|------------------|
| Income | Fixed hourly/project rate | Labor rate + profit share |
| Asset upside | None | Yes — % of net proceeds |
| Decision-making | None | Democratic vote on projects |
| Work pipeline | Depends on GC relationships | Co-op aggregates projects |
| Liability | Personal (if sole prop) | Shared, via entity structure |
| Professional development | Self-funded | Co-op supported |

### The honest obligations

I don't oversell this. Candidates need to understand:

1. **Buy-in capital** — Members contribute initial capital. Amount TBD pending M3 (Ledger). Estimated range for first-round members: likely $5,000–$15,000, structured as equity, not a fee. (Need confirmation from Ledger before putting this in pitch materials.)
2. **Time commitment** — Co-op governance takes time. Monthly meetings minimum. Project participation is expected, not optional.
3. **Shared liability** — Entity structure (TBD from Statton) will shape this, but members share risk in the enterprise.
4. **Variable income** — Co-op project work is not a steady paycheck. Members will likely maintain outside work, especially early.
5. **Democratic process** — Decisions take longer. If you can't handle disagreement-by-vote, this isn't for you.

## What Makes a Portland Tradesperson Say Yes

Based on 8 years working in this community, here's what moves people:

### 1. Respect for their craft and license
Don't pitch co-op membership like you're recruiting a laborer. These are licensed contractors. Address them as professionals. Know their CCB number, know their endorsements, understand their trade.

### 2. A credible financial story
"Profit sharing" is vague. They want numbers. What's the expected net on a flip? What's the labor rate during the project? What's a realistic profit share check for a member who contributes 3 weeks of work? **This needs M3 output from Ledger before we can finalize.**

### 3. Work they can be proud of
Portland tradespeople — especially those with union backgrounds — are deeply allergic to "flipper" culture: fast, cheap, cut corners, sell to out-of-state investors. The co-op needs to lead with quality standards, realistic scopes, and a commitment to doing it right. Birch, Slate, and Copper all exhibit this trait based on their profiles.

### 4. People they'd want to work with
The crew matters as much as the business model. I need to introduce candidates to each other early. Chemistry between Birch, Slate, and Copper is a co-op multiplier.

### 5. Clear answers to "what happens when..."
- What if we lose money on a flip? (Shared loss, limited to buy-in? TBD from Statton/Ledger)
- What if I need to leave? (Equity buyout process — TBD from Statton)
- What if two members disagree? (Dispute resolution — TBD from governance docs)

## Candidate Sourcing — Where to Find Portland Tradespeople

### Tier 1: Direct referral (highest yield)

My best candidates will come from people I already know in the trades network. Birch, Slate, and Copper are in this tier — they're referred. I'll also tap:

- **Pacific NW Regional Council of Carpenters** — I have contacts at the SE Portland hall. Good source for carpenters who are journeyman-level and looking for something more than day-rate framing.
- **UA Local 290** — Copper's union. Journeymen who are close to or at master status, frustrated with the dispatch hall's unpredictability.
- **NRCA Pacific NW chapter** — Slate's world. Small roofing contractors who work solo or with a small crew and want more stability.

### Tier 2: Community organizations

- **Oregon Tradeswomen** — Pre-apprenticeship and apprenticeship pipeline. Important for DEI goals. We're not recruiting pre-apprentices for year one (too junior), but building relationships for year two and beyond.
- **Portland Community College** — Trades programs produce emerging professionals. Same note — too junior for founding membership, good for future associate membership or apprenticeship pipeline.
- **Latino Network / Urban League of Portland** — Trades workers of color who may be operating as informal contractors and ready to formalize with a co-op structure. High potential, needs thoughtful outreach.

### Tier 3: Contractor networks

- **Oregon CCB Licensee Database** — Public. Searchable by trade and location. Good for cold prospecting of licensed sole proprietors in target trades (carpentry, roofing, plumbing).
- **HomeAdvisor/Angi profiles** — Not my first choice, but useful for finding active sole props with real project history and reviews.
- **Portland neighborhood contractor Facebook groups** — Specifically: "Portland Homeowners and Contractors" (approx. 12K members), "PDX Trades Network."

### Tier 4: Events and spaces

- **Portland Building Trades Council events** — Annual gatherings, labor day events, organizing meetings.
- **Portland Housing Bureau outreach events** — Co-op fits the PHB's community development lens; good credibility builder.
- **NE/SE Portland neighborhood association meetings** — Where properties will likely be, where community relationships matter.
- **Portland Saturday Market craftspeople** — Stretch, but woodworkers sometimes know carpenters.

## Vetting Philosophy (preliminary)

Full vetting criteria will go in `vetting-criteria.md`. The short version:

**Non-negotiables:**
- Valid Oregon CCB license (or Oregon plumbing/electrical license for applicable trades)
- Verifiable insurance (GL minimum $1M per occurrence)
- No active CCB complaints or license sanctions
- 3 verifiable professional references
- Willingness to understand (not just accept) the co-op model

**Strong preferences:**
- 8+ years in trade (enough to be self-directing)
- Prior experience working with other trades (not siloed)
- Financial stability sufficient for buy-in (without hardship)
- Some version of "I'm tired of working for people who don't respect the work"

**Red flags:**
- License violations or complaints in CCB database
- Unwillingness to discuss financials
- "I just want the profit share" without valuing the membership obligations
- History of solo-only work with no ability to collaborate

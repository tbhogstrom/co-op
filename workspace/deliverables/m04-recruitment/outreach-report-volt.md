## 2. Key Questions Asked and Responses Given

### Question 1: "Who manages the finances? Is there an accountant?"

I explained the Treasurer role on the Board of Managers, the annual CPA review, the project-level cost tracking, and the fact that all members have visibility into financials at monthly meetings. Told him about the reserve fund (20% of gross profit set aside before any distributions). He nodded along — figuratively. Said "okay, that's better than what I've seen."

### Question 2: "What happens if you buy a house and it's a disaster — foundation problems, asbestos, the whole thing?"

I walked through the acquisition process: BPO appraisal, pre-purchase inspection, scope development with Harlan, and the supermajority vote requirement before committing to buy. Told him any member can vote no on a deal. He liked that. Said he's been on jobs where the GC bought a house without getting the electrical inspected and then handed him a mess. "If I'm at the table before we buy, I can tell you what the panel looks like in 30 minutes."

### Question 3: "What's my actual take on a flip?"

This is where the outreach script paid off. I gave him the numbers: on a conservative first flip at $40,750 gross profit, an electrician putting in 150 hours at the 1.3x trade multiplier would earn roughly $1,600 in labor share, plus a capital share on top. He asked what the labor advance would be during the project. I explained the 50% advance option. He said "so I'm not working for free for six months." Correct.

He then asked a follow-up that showed he was really thinking: "What if I put in 200 hours instead of 150 — does that mean more?" Yes. Your labor share scales with your weighted hours. He said "I could easily put 200 hours into a full rewire on one of those old Lents houses."

### Question 4: "The non-compete — does that mean I can't do my regular electrical work?"

I walked him through the non-compete language exactly: 12-month restriction on flipping houses in Portland metro after departure. His regular electrical business, subcontract work, other GC clients — all explicitly allowed. He said "okay, that's fine. I was worried you were going to tell me I couldn't work for anyone else while I'm in." No.

### Question 5: "How much to join?"

Explained the tiers. He asked about Tier B specifically — $10K to $25K range. Said "I could probably do $15K. I'd need to think about it, but that's doable." This is a strong signal. He's already self-selecting into Tier B without any pressure.

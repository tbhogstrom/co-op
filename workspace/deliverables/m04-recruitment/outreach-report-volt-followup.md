## 3. Questions Raised and Responses Given

### Question 1: "Walk me through the financial model one more time — specifically, what happens if a flip loses money?"

This was his top question. He had read the member-financial-guide and understood the profit-split formula but wanted to know the downside scenario. I was direct: if a project sells below total cost (acquisition + rehab + carry), there is no profit to distribute. The loss reduces the co-op's reserve fund first. If the reserve is insufficient, the loss is allocated to capital accounts proportionally. No member is required to contribute additional capital beyond their buy-in — the LLC structure limits exposure to invested capital.

Volt followed up: "So the worst case is I lose my $15K?" In absolute worst case, yes. But I walked him through the mitigation: the supermajority vote before any acquisition, the pre-purchase inspection process, the conservative scope approach, and the reserve fund (20% of gross profit from every successful flip set aside before distributions). He said: "That's a better structure than most partnerships I've seen."

### Question 2: "The 1.3x trade multiplier for electrical — is that locked in or does it change?"

I explained that the multipliers are set by the operating agreement and can be amended by a supermajority vote of the membership. The 1.3x for electrical and plumbing reflects the licensing requirements, liability exposure, and market rates for those trades relative to general labor. It is not arbitrary — Ledger built it from prevailing wage data and subcontractor rate comparisons.

Volt's response: "Good. Because if someone decides electricians should be at 1.0x after I join, I want to know I have a vote on that." He does. Supermajority means no multiplier change without 75% of members voting yes. As a founding member, his vote would matter.

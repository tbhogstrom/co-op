He can walk into a 1920s bungalow and tell you the plumbing story before he looks at a single pipe.

**Strengths:**
- Union man — 18 years in UA Local 290. He deeply understands collective structures, shared standards, and the idea that workers deserve to benefit from their labor. He will *get* the co-op model quickly.
- Code integrity — Copper will not cut corners. Ever. This is sometimes frustrating on a budget-tight flip, but for a co-op that depends on quality to protect its reputation and margins, this is essential.
- Cully neighborhood (NE Portland) — community roots in a diverse, working-class part of Portland. The co-op's DEI commitments will resonate with him.
- Long-term GC relationships — shows he's collaborative and reliable in an ongoing partnership structure, which is exactly what co-op membership requires.
- Veteran of the commercial-to-residential transition — he has seen both worlds, knows the difference, and chose residential because the relationships mean more.

**Potential friction points:**
- As the most experienced candidate, he may expect deference on technical decisions. That works well in a hierarchical shop; it needs recalibration in a democratic co-op. Will address this directly in the interview: co-op governance means that sometimes the vote goes against you.
- Journeyman (not master) license creates a specific legal question about permit-pulling. Not a dealbreaker, but needs resolution from Statton before we put him on a project.
- Has operated as a sole proprietor for 10 years. He's used to making his own calls on scheduling and prioritization. Co-op coordination will require more communication than he's used to.

**My read:** Copper is the person I'm most confident about as a co-op member. He already thinks in collective terms — it's built into his union background. The financial pitch will land: he knows exactly what GCs make on his labor and he's ready to capture some of that. The process pitch (governance, meetings, collective decisions) will be the part he needs time with. Give him that time. He's worth it.

## 10. Red Flags or Concerns

1. **Journeyman (not master) plumber license.** This is not a dealbreaker — Copper can contract under his CCB and pull permits for most residential plumbing scopes. However, for larger or more complex projects, a master plumber on the team would simplify permitting. Copper is eligible for the master exam and willing to pursue it. Recommend the co-op support this (study materials, exam fee) as an investment.

2. **Most experienced = potential dominance.** At 18 years, Copper is the senior tradesperson in the founding cohort. He may unconsciously expect deference. His union background mitigates this risk significantly — he has lived in democratic structures — but it should be monitored. The operating agreement's one-member-one-vote provision is the structural safeguard.

3. **Sole operator for 10 years.** Like Slate, Copper has been making his own decisions for a decade. Co-op coordination (scheduling, scope changes, collective budget decisions) will require more communication than he is accustomed to. He acknowledged this and seemed genuinely interested in the change: "I'm tired of doing everything alone."

**None of these are disqualifying. None are unexpected. All are manageable.**

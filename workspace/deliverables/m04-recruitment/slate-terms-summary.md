## 1. Exit and Buyout Process

**Voluntary withdrawal** is straightforward. Here is exactly how it works:

| Step | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Notice** | 90 days' written notice to the Board of Managers |
| **Active project hold** | If there is a flip in progress when your 90 days would be up, your exit date extends to 30 days after that project's sale closes. This protects everyone from mid-project disruption. |
| **Payout** | Your full capital account balance as of the exit date — that is your original buy-in plus any allocated but undistributed profit. |
| **Payout timeline** | Within 60 days of the effective exit date. If the co-op is cash-tight, it can pay in installments over 12 months (board's call). |
| **Accrued distributions** | You receive any distributions owed for completed projects you worked on. |
| **Labor advances** | If you received labor advances on an active project, those get reconciled against your final distribution from that project. If advances exceeded your share, you repay the difference within 60 days. |
| **Post-exit claims** | None. After exit, you have no further claim to co-op profits or assets. |

**The bottom line:** You give 90 days' notice, you finish any active project (or wait for it to close), and you get your capital back. No penalty. No forfeiture of what you already earned. The co-op does not keep your money.

### Involuntary Exit (Expulsion)

This is different from voluntary withdrawal. A member can only be expelled for Cause, which requires a two-thirds supermajority vote. "Cause" is defined narrowly:

- Material breach of the agreement (with 30-day cure period first)
- Loss of CCB license
- Lapse of required insurance (with 14-day cure period)
- Felony conviction or crime of dishonesty
- Willful misconduct or gross negligence on a project
- Fraud, theft, or misappropriation
- Safety violations creating substantial risk of injury

Before any expulsion vote, you get: written notice of the grounds (30 days before the vote), the right to submit a written response, and the right to be heard at a members' meeting. Due process.

If expelled, your capital account is returned within 120 days (lump sum) or over 12 months in installments, at the co-op's election, minus any outstanding obligations. You forfeit labor share on any active project that has not yet closed.

## 2. Capital Return Mechanics

Your capital account is not a fee — it is equity. Here is how it works over time:

```
Your Capital Account
  = Initial Buy-In (cash you contribute at joining)
  + Allocated Profit (your capital share from each completed flip)
  - Distributions (cash actually paid out to you)
  - Withdrawals (capital returned if you leave)
  = Current Balance
```

**Key rules:**

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **No interest** | Your capital does not earn interest. Returns come from profit distributions. |
| **Growth** | Your capital account grows when flips are profitable — 30% of each project's gross profit is split among members proportional to their capital account balances. |
| **Non-transferable** | You cannot sell your membership interest to someone else. It can only be redeemed by the co-op. |
| **Minimum reserve protection** | The board can defer a withdrawal if paying it would drop the co-op's cash below $20,000. This protects the co-op's operating capacity. |
| **Death or disability** | Capital account paid to your estate within 120 days, or 12-month installments. |

**What this means at Tier C ($5,000 buy-in):** Your $5,000 goes into your capital account. After each flip, your capital share of the profit gets allocated to that account. If the co-op does well, your account grows beyond $5,000. When you leave, you get the full balance — not just your original $5,000.

## 3. Non-Compete

The non-compete clause is being amended to 12 months (up from 6 months in the original draft). Here is exactly what it covers and what it does not:

**What you cannot do for 12 months after leaving:**
- Buy, renovate, and sell ("flip") residential property in the Portland metro area (Multnomah, Clackamas, Washington counties) for profit

**What you can absolutely still do — during membership and after:**
- Run your own roofing business and serve your own clients
- Subcontract for other GCs
- Own rental properties (passive investment)
- Flip properties outside the Portland metro area
- Work on your personal residence

**Waiver:** The board can grant a case-by-case waiver if you request one. They cannot unreasonably deny it.

**Why it exists:** The co-op's deal pipeline — the properties we identify in Lents, Cully, and Parkrose — is a shared asset. The non-compete prevents a departing member from taking knowledge of that pipeline and competing directly. It is narrowly tailored: limited to one specific activity (flipping), limited geography (Portland metro), and limited duration (12 months).

**Enforceability note from Statton:** This is drafted to be narrow and reasonable under Oregon law. It applies to owner-members, not employees, which gives it a different legal footing than a typical employment non-compete.

## 4. Governance

**One member, one vote.** This is not negotiable and it is not weighted.

| Decision Type | Threshold |
|---------------|-----------|
| Day-to-day operations (budget, policies, vendors, elections) | Majority vote (>50%) |
| Major decisions (new members, property acquisition/sale, debt >$50K, compensation changes) | Supermajority vote (2/3) |
| Fundamental changes (profit formula, dissolution, amendment rules) | Unanimous vote (100%) |

**What this means for you:** With 6 founding members, a majority is 4 votes. A supermajority is 4 votes. Unanimous is 6. No single member — including Maven, who is putting in $40,000 — can override the group. Your $5,000 vote counts exactly the same as a $40,000 vote.

**Board of Managers:** The board runs day-to-day operations. Board members are elected by majority vote. Any working member can serve. The board includes a President, Treasurer, and Secretary at minimum.

**Monthly meetings:** Members meet monthly. You participate in project selection, budget review, and policy decisions. Between meetings, the board handles operations.

## 5. Buy-In Options

| Tier | Amount | What You Get |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Tier C (Sweat Equity Entry)** | **$5,000** | Full membership. Full voting rights. Full labor share. Full capital share (proportional to your $5K). This is the minimum entry. |
| **Tier B (Working Share)** | $10,000 - $24,999 | Same rights as Tier C. Larger capital share means larger slice of the 30% capital distribution on each flip. |
| **Tier A (Full Share)** | $25,000 - $50,000 | Same rights. Largest capital share. |

**There is only one class of membership.** Tier C members have exactly the same voting power and labor share access as Tier A members. The only difference is the size of your capital share distribution.

**No payment plans for founding members.** Buy-in is due in full at membership. (Future members may get installment options — founding members do not.)

These are estimates based on conservative numbers. Actual results depend on project profitability and your labor contribution. But the structure is designed so that even at the minimum buy-in, your return on capital is strong — because the labor share is where most of the money flows, and that is based on your hours, not your capital.

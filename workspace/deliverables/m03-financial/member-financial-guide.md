# How the Money Works

**Portland Housing Co-op -- Member Financial Guide**

This guide explains how you earn money, how you get paid, and what you owe in taxes. Read it before your first project. Keep it handy. If something does not make sense, ask Ledger (CFO) or Maven (Operations).

---

## Your Capital Account

When you join, you put money in. That money goes into your **capital account** -- think of it as your equity stake in the co-op.

- **Minimum buy-in:** $5,000
- **Maximum buy-in:** $50,000
- **No payment plans** for founding members.

Your capital account goes up when the co-op makes money (your share of profits gets added). It goes down if you take money out or if a project loses money.

**Your capital account is not a bank account.** You cannot withdraw whenever you want. If you want your capital back, you give 90 days written notice, and it cannot happen during an active project. The board can also delay withdrawal if it would drain the co-op's reserves below $20,000.

Your capital earns no interest. Returns come from doing flips, not from sitting on capital.

---

## The Profit Split: 20/10/30/40

When we sell a flipped house, the gross profit (sale price minus all costs) gets split four ways:

| Bucket | % | Where it goes |
|--------|---|---------------|
| Reserves | 20% | Stays in the co-op. Funds the next deal, covers emergencies. |
| Overhead | 10% | Stays in the co-op. Pays for insurance, tools, admin. |
| Capital Share | 30% | Split among members based on how much capital each person has in. |
| Labor Share | 40% | Split among members based on hours worked on this project. |

**The biggest slice goes to labor.** The more hours you put in, the more you earn. But capital matters too -- the 30% capital share rewards the people who funded the deal.

### Worked Example

Say we flip a house and make $30,700 in gross profit. Here is how it splits:

**Step 1: Split into buckets**

| Bucket | Calculation | Amount |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Reserves | $30,700 x 20% | $6,140 |
| Overhead | $30,700 x 10% | $3,070 |
| Capital pool | $30,700 x 30% | $9,210 |
| Labor pool | $30,700 x 40% | $12,280 |

**Step 2: Capital share** (based on how much each member has in)

Maven put in $50,000 of the co-op's $200,000 total capital = 25%.
Maven's capital share = $9,210 x 25% = $2,303.

Member F put in $25,000 = 12.5%.
Member F's capital share = $9,210 x 12.5% = $1,151.

**Step 3: Labor share** (based on weighted hours on this project)

Copper worked 350 hours of plumbing (1.3x multiplier) = 455 weighted hours.
The team logged 2,120 total weighted hours.
Copper's share = $12,280 x (455 / 2,120) = $2,636.

Member F worked 300 hours of painting (1.0x multiplier) = 300 weighted hours.
Member F's share = $12,280 x (300 / 2,120) = $1,739.

**Step 4: Total payout**

| Member | Capital Share | Labor Share | Total |
|--------|-------------|------------|-------|
| Maven | $2,303 | $1,134 | $3,437 |
| Member B | $1,612 | $2,581 | $4,193 |
| Member C | $1,382 | $2,724 | $4,106 |
| Member D | $1,382 | $2,043 | $3,425 |
| Member E | $1,382 | $1,844 | $3,226 |
| Member F | $1,151 | $1,704 | $2,855 |

If you drew any advances during the project, those get subtracted from your payout.

## How Labor Hours Are Tracked

You log your hours every day -- same day, no batching.

- Round to the nearest 15 minutes (0.25 hours).
- Minimum loggable block: 1 hour.
- One entry per trade type per day. If you did plumbing in the morning and general labor in the afternoon, that is two entries.
- Write what you actually did. "Installed drain lines in master bath" -- not "worked on house."
- Travel time does not count.

**The PM verifies all hours weekly.** If there is a dispute, the board decides. Their call is final.

### Why Multipliers Matter

Not all hours count the same. Licensed trades that command higher market rates get a multiplier:

| Trade | Multiplier | What it means |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| General labor, painting | 1.0x | 8 hours = 8.0 weighted hours |
| Project management | 1.15x | 8 hours = 9.2 weighted hours |
| Carpentry, framing, roofing | 1.2x | 8 hours = 9.6 weighted hours |
| Plumbing, electrical, HVAC | 1.3x | 8 hours = 10.4 weighted hours |

You do not get paid a dollar rate per hour. The multiplier adjusts your share of the labor pool. A plumber who works 350 hours gets a bigger slice than a painter who works 350 hours, because the plumber's weighted hours are higher.

If you work in multiple trades on a project (say, carpentry and general labor), each block of hours gets its own multiplier.

## Advances: Getting Paid Before the Flip Sells

Rehab projects take months. If you need cash before the sale closes, you can draw an advance against your expected labor share.

**Rules:**

1. **Not available until the project is 30% complete.** No draws in the first month.
2. **Maximum: 50% of your estimated labor share.** The estimate uses conservative numbers -- not the best-case scenario.
3. **Board must approve each draw.** (The Treasurer can approve draws up to $3,000/month on their own.)
4. **It gets deducted from your final payout.** An advance is not extra money -- it is early money.

**Example:** Your estimated labor share on a project is $2,700. Your max advance is $2,700 x 50% = $1,350. If you draw $1,000 during rehab, your final payout is reduced by $1,000.

**What if the project earns less than expected?** If your advance exceeds your actual labor share, you owe the co-op the difference. It gets deducted from your capital account. If your capital account does not cover it, you have 60 days to repay in cash.

This almost never happens if we stick to the 50% cap on conservative estimates. But it is the rule, and you should know it.

## Taxes

The co-op is a pass-through entity. It does not pay income tax. You do.

### What You Will Receive

After each fiscal year, you get a **Schedule K-1** showing your share of the co-op's income. This goes on your personal tax return. The co-op aims to deliver K-1s within 75 days of year-end (by mid-March).

### What You Owe

- **Federal income tax** on your share of co-op income, at your personal rate.
- **Self-employment tax** (Social Security + Medicare) -- currently 15.3% on the first ~$160,000 of self-employment income. On a $4,000 distribution, that is roughly $612 in SE tax alone.
- **Oregon state income tax** at your marginal rate.

### Quarterly Estimated Payments

You are responsible for making quarterly estimated tax payments to the IRS (Form 1040-ES) and Oregon (Form OR-40-V). If you do not, you will owe penalties at tax time.

**Suggested holdback:** Set aside 25-30% of every distribution for taxes. If your first flip nets you $3,500, put $900-$1,050 in a savings account for taxes before you spend anything.

The co-op will provide estimated K-1 projections after each flip so you can plan your quarterly payments.

### Important

The co-op does not withhold taxes from your distributions. You are not an employee. You are an owner. Managing your own taxes is part of the deal.

If you have never filed self-employment taxes before, talk to a tax professional before your first K-1 arrives. This is not optional advice.

## What Happens If You Want to Leave

1. Give 90 days written notice to the board.
2. You cannot withdraw during an active project. If a project is underway, your withdrawal is deferred until 30 days after the sale closes.
3. You get your capital account balance paid out -- your original buy-in plus any accumulated profits, minus any distributions already taken and any outstanding advances.
4. Payment happens within 60 days, or in installments over 12 months if a lump sum would strain the co-op.
5. You get your share of any completed projects where you worked but have not yet been paid.
6. After the effective date, you have no further claim on co-op profits or assets.

## What Happens If a Project Loses Money

It is possible. If a flip sells for less than it cost:

- The **reserves and overhead buckets get nothing.** There is no profit to split.
- The loss is charged to members' **capital accounts** in proportion to their capital percentage. If you have 25% of capital, you absorb 25% of the loss.
- **You do not write a check.** The loss reduces your capital account balance on paper. No one makes a cash payment.
- If you drew advances on the losing project, those advances become a debt to the co-op. They get deducted from your capital account. If your capital account cannot cover it, you have 60 days to repay.

**Example:** A project loses $10,000. You have 15% of capital. Your capital account drops by $1,500 (from $30,000 to $28,500). You drew $800 in advances. That $800 is also deducted, bringing you to $27,700. No cash changes hands -- but your equity in the co-op is lower.

This is why we have strict deal standards (buy at 65% or less of after-repair value, target 15%+ ROI). The underwriting protects everyone's capital.

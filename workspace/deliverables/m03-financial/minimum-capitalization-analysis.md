# Portland Housing Co-op — Minimum Viable Capitalization Analysis

**Author:** Ledger (CFO)
**Date:** 2026-04-08
**Status:** DRAFT — M3 Deliverable
**Context:** Founding cohort dashboard projects $115K (moderate scenario) vs. $200K target

---

## 1. The Question

The founding cohort status dashboard projects $115K in member equity under the moderate recruitment scenario. Our capital structure was designed around a $200K target. Can we proceed at $115K, and what are the trade-offs?

---

## 2. Minimum Equity Required per Acquisition (85% LTV Hard Money)

With 85% LTV hard money financing, the co-op needs to cover:

- **15% down payment** on purchase price
- **20-30% of rehab budget** (hard money covers 70-80%)
- **Acquisition closing costs** (origination points, inspection, title, etc.)
- **Carrying cost float** (interest and holding costs during rehab, before draw reimbursements)
- **Operating reserve** (3 months overhead = ~$14,600)

### Per-Property Equity Requirements

| Component | $180K Property | $220K Property | $260K Property |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| Down payment (15%) | $27,000 | $33,000 | $39,000 |
| Rehab gap (25% of $60K budget) | $15,000 | $15,000 | $15,000 |
| Acquisition costs (origination 2.5 pts + title/inspection) | $6,500 | $7,500 | $8,500 |
| Carrying cost float (2 months at ~$1,800/mo) | $3,600 | $4,200 | $4,800 |
| **Subtotal: Project equity needed** | **$52,100** | **$59,700** | **$67,300** |
| Operating reserve (minimum) | $14,600 | $14,600 | $14,600 |
| Contingency reserve (15% of rehab) | $9,000 | $9,000 | $9,000 |
| **Total equity needed to start** | **$75,700** | **$83,300** | **$90,900** |

### Answer: Minimum member equity for first acquisition = ~$76K-$91K

This varies by purchase price. For the low end of our target range ($180K in Lents/Parkrose), $76K gets us in the door. For the upper end ($260K in Cully), we need $91K.

## 3. Does $115K Work?

**Yes, but with constraints.**

| Scenario | $180K Purchase | $220K Purchase | $260K Purchase |
|----------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| Equity needed | $75,700 | $83,300 | $90,900 |
| Available equity | $115,000 | $115,000 | $115,000 |
| **Surplus / (Deficit)** | **$39,300** | **$31,700** | **$24,100** |
| Surplus as % of project equity | 51.9% | 38.1% | 26.5% |

At $115K, the co-op can fund one acquisition at any price point in our target range. The surplus provides a buffer for cost overruns but is thin at the $260K level.

### What $115K Buys

- One flip at a time (sequential, not parallel)
- Adequate reserves for a $180K-$220K property
- Tight but workable reserves for a $260K property
- No ability to start a second project until the first one sells

### What $115K Does NOT Buy

- Parallel flips (need ~$160K+ for overlapping projects)
- Comfortable cushion for a worst-case rehab overrun on a $260K property
- Rapid scaling in Year 1 (stuck at 2 flips/year maximum with sequential timing)

## 5. Risk Assessment at $115K

### Risks We Can Manage

1. **Rehab budget overruns.** At $115K with a $180K acquisition, we have ~$39K surplus beyond minimum requirements. A 25% rehab overrun ($15K on a $60K budget) is absorbable. Mitigation: conservative scoping, detailed SOW before acquisition, contingency line items.

2. **Extended holding period.** If a flip takes 8 months instead of 5, additional carrying costs are ~$5K-$7K. Absorbable from surplus. Mitigation: price the property to sell, don't get greedy on ARV.

3. **Lower-than-expected sale price.** A 5% miss on a $280K ARV target = $14K less gross profit. The co-op doesn't lose money on the flip, but member distributions shrink. Reserves absorb the hit.

### Risks That Could Hurt

4. **Catastrophic rehab discovery** (e.g., foundation issue, hidden mold, sewer line). Could add $20K-$40K to a project. At $115K, a $40K surprise on a $220K purchase would burn through all surplus and require either: (a) additional member contributions, (b) bridge financing from hard money lender (if available), or (c) selling at a loss. At $200K, this is painful but survivable.

5. **Market correction during hold period.** Portland's east-side neighborhoods are relatively insulated (lower price points, strong rental demand), but a 10% market decline on a $280K ARV means selling at $252K -- potentially a break-even or loss scenario on a $220K purchase. At $115K, one break-even flip could delay the second flip by months while reserves rebuild.

6. **Member departure during active project.** If a member exits and requests capital return, the co-op may not have liquidity to honor the withdrawal while a project is in progress. The 90-day notice period and prohibition on withdrawal during active projects (per OA) mitigates this, but at $115K the balance sheet is tight.

### Risk Rating

| Risk Level | $115K | $200K |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Single normal flip | LOW | LOW |
| Single flip + moderate overrun | LOW-MEDIUM | LOW |
| Single flip + major overrun | MEDIUM-HIGH | MEDIUM |
| Two sequential flips Year 1 | MEDIUM | LOW |
| Market downturn during hold | HIGH | MEDIUM |

## 6. Recommendation

**Proceed at $115K with guardrails.** Waiting for $200K delays launch by an estimated 3-6 months and risks losing momentum with committed members. The numbers work if we discipline our first acquisition.

### Guardrails for a $115K Launch

1. **First acquisition ceiling: $200K purchase price.** Do not stretch to $260K on the first flip. Target Lents or Parkrose at $180K-$200K where the equity cushion is thickest.

2. **Rehab budget ceiling: $55K on first flip.** Keep it cosmetic-plus (kitchen, bath, paint, flooring, landscaping). Avoid structural or major-system projects until the balance sheet is stronger.

3. **Pre-acquisition reserve lockup: $23,600.** Before closing on the first property, verify that $23,600 ($14,600 operating reserve + $9,000 contingency at 15% of $60K rehab) is set aside and untouchable.

4. **No parallel projects until reserves hit $40K.** After the first flip, reinvest reserves aggressively but do not start project 2 until the reserve fund can support it.

5. **Continue recruiting during first flip.** If we can add 1-2 members during the 5-6 month flip cycle, their buy-ins go directly to the balance sheet and expand capacity for flip 2.

6. **Scope inspection contingency.** On the first acquisition, budget $2,000 for a comprehensive inspection (general + sewer scope + structural). Walk away from any property with surprises that could blow the rehab budget past $55K.

### Bottom Line

$115K is enough to start. It is not enough to be comfortable. The first flip needs to be a disciplined, conservative play -- a $180K-$200K house in Lents or Parkrose with a cosmetic rehab and a realistic ARV. If we execute that flip cleanly, the co-op will have ~$130K-$145K in equity (original capital + retained reserves + profit) and the track record to attract additional members and better financing.

The risk of waiting for $200K is real: member fatigue, market movement, and lost time. The risk of starting at $115K is also real: one bad flip with no cushion. The guardrails above thread that needle.

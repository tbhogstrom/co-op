## 1. Entity & Tax Framework

**Entity:** Cooperative LLC organized under ORS Chapter 63
**Tax treatment:** Pass-through (Form 1065, K-1s to members)
**Fiscal year:** Calendar year (Jan 1 – Dec 31)
**Accounting method:** Cash basis (simpler for Year 1; can convert to accrual when scaling)

### Why Cooperative LLC?

- LLC flexibility with cooperative governance principles written into the Operating Agreement
- Pass-through taxation avoids double taxation
- One member, one vote governance (regardless of capital share)
- Patronage-based distributions align with our labor-weighted profit split
- Oregon LLC annual fee is $100 (vs. cooperative corporation which has more filing overhead)

## 2. Capital Accounts

### Structure

Each member has a **capital account** tracked by the co-op. This is their equity stake.

```
Member Capital Account
├── Initial Buy-In (cash contribution at joining)
├── Additional Contributions (optional, board-approved)
├── Allocated Profit (capital share of each flip's profit)
├── Less: Distributions (cash paid out to member)
├── Less: Withdrawals (capital returned to member)
└── = Current Balance
```

### Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Minimum buy-in** | $5,000 |
| **Maximum buy-in** | $50,000 |
| **Payment plans** | Not available for founding members; may be offered to future members |
| **Additional contributions** | Accepted with board approval; adjusts capital percentages for future flips |
| **Interest on capital** | None. Returns come from profit distributions, not interest. |
| **Withdrawal** | 90-day written notice. Not permitted during active projects. Subject to minimum reserve balance. Board approval required. |
| **Involuntary redemption** | If a member is expelled per OA terms, capital account balance is returned within 180 days, less any outstanding obligations (advances, damages). |
| **Death/disability** | Capital account balance is payable to estate within 180 days. |
| **Transfer** | Capital accounts are non-transferable except to the co-op. No secondary market. |

## 3. Equity Classes

The co-op has one class of membership equity. There are no preferred shares, no silent
investor class, and no debt-to-equity conversion rights. This is intentional — it keeps
governance simple and prevents capital from dominating labor in decision-making.

### What Capital Buys You

1. **Pro-rata share of the capital portion (30%) of each flip's profit**
2. **Priority return of capital** if the co-op dissolves (capital accounts returned before any surplus is distributed)
3. **One vote** on all co-op decisions (same as every other member, regardless of amount invested)

### What Capital Does NOT Buy You

1. No guaranteed return
2. No interest payments
3. No extra votes
4. No veto power
5. No priority on labor opportunities

## 4. Debt Capacity

### Year 1: Hard Money Only

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Lender type | Hard money / private lender |
| LTV | 85% of purchase price |
| Rehab funding | 70-80% of rehab budget (drawn as work completes) |
| Interest rate | 10.5-12% annual |
| Origination | 2-3 points |
| Term | 6-12 months (bridge loan) |
| Collateral | The property being flipped |
| Personal guarantees | May be required — Statton to advise on limiting to managing member(s) |

**Key point:** Hard money is project-level debt, not co-op-level debt. The co-op itself
does not carry long-term debt in Year 1. Each flip has its own loan that is paid off
at sale.

### Year 2-3: Transition Path

| Stage | Debt Type | Trigger |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| After 2 successful flips | Establish banking relationship; apply for business line of credit | Track record + tax returns |
| After 4 flips | Conventional investment property loans (lower rates, 75% LTV) | 2 years of financials |
| At scale (5+/yr) | Warehouse line of credit for multiple simultaneous projects | Audited financials |

The transition from hard money to conventional lending drops carrying costs by 40-50%
and is the single biggest lever for improving per-flip ROI at scale.

## 5. Reserve Requirements

### Reserve Fund Structure

```
Reserve Fund
├── Operating Reserve (3 months overhead)     $14,600
├── Project Contingency Reserve               Variable (15% of next flip's rehab budget)
├── Growth Reserve (for next flip down payment) Variable
└── Total Target (end of Year 1)              $15,000 - $25,000
```

### Funding Mechanism

- 20% of gross profit from each flip goes to reserves (per approved profit split)
- Conservative first flip: 20% × $30,700 = $6,140
- Moderate first flip: 20% × $59,762 = $11,952
- Target: Accumulate $15K-$25K in reserves after first flip

### Reserve Policy

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Minimum operating reserve** | 3 months of fixed overhead (~$14,600). Do not start a new project if reserves are below this level. |
| **Project contingency** | 15% of the next project's rehab budget must be in reserves before acquisition. |
| **Board authorization** | Any draw from reserves >$5,000 requires majority board vote. |
| **Replenishment** | If reserves are drawn below minimum, next flip's reserve allocation increases to 25% until replenished. |
| **Investment** | Reserve funds held in FDIC-insured high-yield savings. No speculative investment of reserves. |

## 6. Cash Management

### Bank Accounts

| Account | Purpose | Signatories |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Operating Checking** | Day-to-day expenses, overhead payments | Any 2 of 3 authorized members |
| **Reserve Savings** | Reserve fund (separate from operating) | Board resolution required for withdrawals |
| **Project Trust** | Per-project funds during active flip | Any 2 of 3 authorized members |

### Cash Flow Governance

1. **Dual signature** on all disbursements >$2,500
2. **Monthly financial statements** prepared by Ledger, reviewed by board
3. **Weekly cash position report** during active projects
4. **No commingling** of project funds with operating funds
5. **No personal expenses** through co-op accounts

## 8. Tax Considerations (Preliminary — Statton to Confirm)

### Pass-Through Taxation

- Co-op files Form 1065 (partnership return)
- Each member receives K-1 with their share of income/loss
- Members pay tax on their individual returns
- **Oregon pass-through entity tax (PTE):** The co-op may elect to pay Oregon income tax at the entity level (currently 9%) and members claim a credit. This can be advantageous for members in higher brackets. Statton should evaluate.

### Self-Employment Tax

- Members' distributive share of co-op income is likely subject to self-employment tax (15.3% on first ~$160K)
- This is a significant cost — on a $5,000 distribution, ~$765 goes to SE tax
- Statton should evaluate whether an S-corp election could reduce SE tax exposure at scale

### Estimated Tax Payments

- Members should make quarterly estimated tax payments
- The co-op should provide estimated K-1 projections after each flip so members can plan
- Consider withholding estimated taxes from distributions (10-15% suggested holdback)
